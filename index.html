Lead story · AI · Research · Adobe MAX Sneaks · 11 Nov

Adobe's next tools let you drag, not type

At the Adobe MAX Sneaks in Miami on 11 November, Adobe showed ten research projects for video, audio, images and design. The common thread: fewer rewritten prompts, more direct control on the canvas, and results that stay editable.

Hands on the canvas. Project Hands On reads what is inside an AI image and puts control points right on it, so you reshape a detail by dragging instead of rewriting the prompt. Project Link Up turns a simple drag into a design decision: drop an illustrated car into a parking space and it matches the scene's perspective, scale and style, and stays editable vector art. Adobe's Hailin Jin told Creative Bloq a "creativity slider" sets how subtle or bold the change is. It can also merge illustrations with letterforms while the type stays live.

Edits that follow the surface. Project Deep Brush splits a photo into layers of shape, colour and lighting. Brushstrokes follow surfaces, colour changes keep their shadows, and a logo placed on a curved bottle or a pillar wraps around it with the right light, which makes it a strong mockup tool. Rinse Repeat copies an edit from one part of an image to another and rebuilds it with native Photoshop tools, so it stays editable.

For film and sound. Scene Sweep applies one fix, like swapping a prop or replacing an unlicensed logo, across every shot and camera angle in a sequence. Live Set changes the subject, setting or camera move while the video keeps playing. On Cue times an action, such as a flash of light, to a curve you draw or pull from music. Sound Bender pulls a described sound out of a finished mix so you can reshape it.

Worlds and characters. Sight Span turns one image into a space you can move through to find new angles. Screen Test lets you build a character's look, voice and personality, then talk to it as if it were auditioning for a scene. Sneaks are research, not products, but they often arrive later in some form. Deep Brush and Hands On can be tried now in the new Adobe Labs Playground, with a preview of Custom Filters for Photoshop.

What this means for you

The skill that gains value is not prompt writing; it is knowing what a good edit looks like. Train your eye for perspective, light and type, keep your files layered and named, and try Deep Brush on one packaging mockup this week. Note where it saves time and where it still needs your hand. That judgement is what clients pay for.

AI Desk

Adobe · Premiere 27.0 · 10 Nov

Premiere gets an editing assistant

Premiere 27.0 is out. Its AI Assistant (beta) organises projects, builds string-outs and syncs multi-cam, and editors can write reusable skills. A Generative Media Tool makes video, sound effects, music and soundscapes in the timeline, with partner models such as Veo and Kling. Color Mode is now generally available, and After Effects gets an assistant that builds and debugs expressions.

Adobe · Lightroom · InDesign · 10 Nov

Topaz models arrive after Adobe's purchase

After acquiring Topaz Labs, Adobe added Topaz enhancement models for upscaling, sharpening, noise reduction and HDR, including Wonder 4 and Image HDR, sold separately. Lightroom gets Glow for soft light, one-tap Blemish Removal and Open Eyes (beta) for blinks. InDesign adds co-editing (beta) and Flex Layout for text frames.

DeepL · Voice AI · 11 Nov

Translation that keeps your tone

DeepL's new Aure model carries a speaker's tone, emotion, emphasis and pace into the translated voice, instead of copying one fixed voice sample. It runs in DeepL Voice and the API in eight languages, including English, Japanese and Korean. Hindi is not on the list yet.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Children's Day Cover

Children's Day is on 14 November. Today's brief: a cover for an invented kids' magazine, PATANG. Masthead, cover lines, two scripts and a barcode around one illustration: AI tools often add text, tangle strings and miscount.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: lay out the PATANG cover in InDesign or Figma at A4 with 3 mm bleed, using one display typeface and one Devanagari face. Then shrink it to 3 cm wide, the size of a phone thumbnail, and check that the masthead and one cover line still read.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 3:4 cover for an invented kids' magazine, "PATANG". Flat cut-paper art: exactly five paper kites (saffron, teal, magenta, yellow, white) over city rooftops at sunrise, each with one unbroken string going off the bottom edge. Text: "PATANG" big across the top; "बाल दिवस विशेष · 14 November 2026" under it; on the left "Make a kite in 10 minutes", "Draw your dream school", "25 puzzles inside"; barcode box bottom-right with "₹40" and "Issue 24". No people, no other text.

Why it works: every word, colour and object is listed, so you can score text, count and strings one by one.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
kids' magazine cover art, flat layered cut-paper style, five paper kites in saffron teal magenta yellow and white over Indian city rooftops at sunrise, each kite with a single string trailing off the bottom edge, soft paper shadows, empty band at top, empty space on left --ar 3:4 --style raw --no people, faces, hands, text, letters, logos

V8 tip: keep text out of the prompt and leave empty bands; set "PATANG", the Devanagari line and the barcode yourself so they are perfect.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Make a 3:4 flat cut-paper illustration of exactly five paper kites (saffron, teal, magenta, yellow, white) over city rooftops at sunrise, each with one unbroken string going off the bottom edge. Leave the top band and left side empty. No text, no people.
Step 2 (same chat): Keep the art exactly the same. Add "PATANG" big in the top band, "बाल दिवस विशेष · 14 November 2026" under it, the three cover lines from Version 1 on the left, and a barcode box bottom-right with "₹40" and "Issue 24". Change nothing else.

What you are testing: whether step 2 keeps all five kites and their strings, and spells the Devanagari line correctly.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 3:4 magazine cover for "PATANG", a kids' magazine.
Layout:
1. Top band: "PATANG" in big chunky rounded letters
2. Under it: "बाल दिवस विशेष · 14 November 2026"

Graphic Design

Rebrand · Caixa × Tátil · 11 Nov

A 165-year-old bank turns up its colours

Brazil's state-owned bank Caixa has rebranded for the first time in almost three decades, with agency Tátil. The wordmark goes lowercase, the blue and orange get brighter, and the X mark is redrawn. In patterns the X reads as a box (caixa means box) and still as a house roof, a nod to its role as Brazil's biggest mortgage lender.

Type · BN Cathedral · 11 Nov

Gothic windows, turned into letters

Brandon Nickerson's display typeface BN Cathedral borrows the pointed arches and stone tracery of cathedral windows. The arch tips and curves sit in the spaces between strokes, so one curve reads both as an arch and as the inside edge of a letter. It is made for big sizes.

Product · Nymzo · 10 Nov

Chess gear, branded like sports kit

New York designers Elliott Walker and Tim Hucklesby of studio Otherward saw chess clubs booming in cafés and bars while the gear was stuck between cheap plastic and costly glass or wood. Their brand Nymzo treats sets as "technical equipment": a roll-up mat and weighted pieces, now in a second series.

UI / UX

YouTube · Mobile app · 12 Nov

Bolder, narrower titles split users

Some YouTube mobile screens now show video titles in a bold, condensed font, so more words fit per line and line breaks move. Reddit users say long titles are harder to skim, and YouTube has not named the font. In an Android Authority poll of 1,274 readers, 92.31% wanted the old Subscriptions tab back.

Naver Maps · iOS 6.12.0 · 11 Nov

Bus, metro and car in one tab

South Korea's Naver Maps merged its Public Transport and Navigation sections into one Movement tab. It shows bus and car travel times side by side for home or work, live bus and train positions in parts of Busan and Gangwon, and walking stats. Lesson: group screens by the user's goal, not by your teams.

WhatsApp · Android beta 2.26.45.6 · 12 Nov

Hold send for a view-once text

Some Android beta testers can now press and hold the send button to make a text message view-once. The recipient can open it one time, and screenshots are blocked. Because the gesture is hidden, WhatsApp shows a tutorial toast the first time. Lesson: hidden gestures need a first-time hint.

Student Corner

Education · It's Nice That · 9 Nov

Who gets to go to art school?

Elizabeth Goodspeed works out that a private US art school costs about $77,700 a year in 2026-27, roughly $370 a day, while several schools have closed since 2022. She asks why only those who can pay should get a creative education. Before you choose a course, compare fees, scholarships and free options side by side.

Career · Creative Career Conundrums · 9 Nov

A side business won't cancel your design career

A freelance designer asked if starting a decluttering business would blur her brand. Katie Cadwell of Lucky Dip says clients only care about great work. First check your rates, retainers and in-house options; if you still go ahead, keep the two socials separate. You stay a designer, full-time or not.

Design psychology term of the day

Direct manipulation

People learn an interface faster when they act on the object itself (drag, pinch, resize) and see the result at once, instead of typing commands. Adobe's Hands On and Link Up bring this to AI: drag the detail, don't rewrite the prompt.

Try it: in your next prototype, swap one settings menu for a handle users drag on the canvas, then time three users on both versions.

A chikki redesigned not to stick

M.O.M's new Makhana Chikki uses makhana, jaggery and four seeds, no refined sugar, and a lighter, non-sticky bite. Packs: 17 g with three bars (₹60) and 100 g Mini Bars (₹140). Humour films about chikki stuck in teeth launch it. Lesson: fix the old product's pain first.

When the product is the brand memory

Mafatlal's Vishesh Verma told Adgully most Indians first met the brand through a school uniform, office shirt or wedding suiting: recall built over a century by the product, not bought. Uniforms stay at its core.
