Lead story · UX ethics · Retail · 8 Nov

When "Agree" gets tapped for you

Some Apple Store staff in the US allegedly signed shoppers up for auto-renewing subscription trials without clear consent, MacRumors reports. Apple has dropped a 40% sales target and reminded staff about consent. It is a sharp lesson in how a business target can quietly shape an interface.

How the flow works. Consumer-rights advocate Louis Rossman shared YouTube videos over two weeks, based on tips from staff. A customer scans a "Connect and Personalize" QR code. The employee's device then shows the customer's Apple devices, current subscriptions and eligible offers, with "Agree" and "Decline" buttons. The saved payment method on the Apple Account is ready to use by default.

The target behind it. Until early last week, at least some US stores tracked a "services attach rate" with a 40% goal: four of every ten buyers should add a subscription. Tipsters say some staff rushed the explanation, called trials "gifts", or even tapped "Agree" themselves.

Apple's response. MacRumors confirmed the metric was removed. Apple's stated reason is that it matters less in the holiday season, when people buy for others. Later in the week, an internal memo reminded staff to respect privacy and get consent, including for trials, Apple Upgrade sign-ups and Genius Bar work.

The design gap. Apple does email customers when a trial starts, but that email is easy to miss. The bigger issue is that the choice lived on a screen the customer did not hold, behind a button anyone nearby could press. You can check trials in Settings, under your name, then Subscriptions.

What this means for you

Consent is a design decision, not only a legal one. The person who pays should make the choice on their own screen, and see the price, renewal date and how to cancel before "Agree". When you design sign-up flows for Indian apps and D2C brands, ask the client which target the screen must hit. If a sales metric is setting the defaults, design friction back in.

An AI detector that shows where the AI is

Pangram Image 1.0 scored 99.96% on the company's own test of 1,200 AI copies of 100 real photos, and draws a heatmap over edited areas. A faster Flash 1.0 model checks video frame by frame, and a Chrome extension scans your feed. These are company figures, so test it on your own work first.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Bhai Dooj Sticker Pack

Diwali week ends with Bhai Dooj, and half of India will wish their siblings on WhatsApp. Today's brief is a sticker set: six stickers of the same two invented characters. Character consistency across a set is still where most AI tools slip.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: draw the same six stickers in Illustrator or Procreate, export them as 512 × 512 WebP files with transparent backgrounds, and try them in WhatsApp. Then compare: which looks more like one family, yours or the AI set?

Version 1 · Any tool
A square sticker sheet for an invented sticker brand, CHITTHI. Six separate die-cut stickers in a 2 × 3 grid on a plain light-grey background, each with a thick white border. Same two cartoon characters in every sticker: Tara, an older sister with a long plait and a mustard-yellow kurta, and Tikku, a younger brother with round glasses and a blue kurta. Stickers: 1) Tara applying a tilak, text "Tilak time!" 2) Tikku holding a box of sweets 3) both hugging, text "भाई दूज" 4) Tikku handing over a small gift 5) Tara laughing with one clay diya 6) Tikku with a heart, text "Love you, Didi". Flat vector illustration, bold outlines, warm marigold, pink and blue palette, no 3D, no other text.

Why it works: it fixes the grid, the border and two clear character "anchors" (plait, glasses, kurta colours), so you can check consistency sticker by sticker.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
sticker sheet, six die-cut cartoon stickers in a 2x3 grid, thick white borders, plain light grey background, same two characters in every sticker: older sister with long plait and mustard-yellow kurta, younger brother with round glasses and blue kurta, tilak, sweets box, hug, small gift, clay diya, heart, flat vector, bold outlines, marigold pink and blue palette --ar 1:1 --style raw --no 3d, gradients, deities, logos

V8 tip: leave the Hindi and English lines out of the prompt and add them in Illustrator. Use one sticker you like as an image reference, then re-run to tighten consistency.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a character sheet of two flat vector cartoon siblings on white: Tara, older sister, long plait, mustard-yellow kurta; Tikku, younger brother, round glasses, blue kurta. Front view, bold outlines.
Step 2 (same chat): Using exactly these two characters, make a square sheet of 6 die-cut stickers in a 2 × 3 grid on light grey, thick white borders: tilak with "Tilak time!", sweets box, hug with "भाई दूज", gift, one clay diya, heart with "Love you, Didi". Keep faces, plait, glasses and kurta colours identical.

What you are testing: whether a character sheet in step 1 keeps the siblings consistent in step 2, and whether the Hindi stays correct beside English.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 1:1 sticker sheet for "CHITTHI".
Layout:
1. Background: plain light grey
2. Grid: 2 rows × 3 stickers, evenly spaced
3. Each sticker: thick white die-cut border
4. Characters in all 6: Tara (long plait, yellow kurta), Tikku (round glasses, blue kurta)
5. Stickers: tilak "Tilak time!" / sweets box / hug "भाई दूज" / gift / one clay diya / heart "Love you, Didi"
6. Small "CHITTHI" wordmark bottom centre
Flat vector, bold outlines, no 3D, no other text.

Tip: if one sticker drifts, reply "redo only sticker 4, same characters and outfits, change nothing else" instead of regenerating the sheet.

Some of the smartest GTA VI ads in Miami show no game at all. Billboards and kiosks promote its parody brands, like Crowex watches and Beige shapewear, with just a small logo and QR code. Creative Bloq says showing the setting, not the product, beats game-ad fatigue.

Studio Play built the identity for Simmer, a game where fans form virtual teams, from ancient stone carvings of a hunter and a lion facing off. The angular shapes run into type, layout and motion, and a warm palette saves cyan for the key stats.

São Paulo studio Polar, two of whose founders are partners in the bakery, built a small type toolkit instead of a fixed logo. When P.A.C. moved to a bigger space, the identity grew with it rather than being redesigned from scratch.

In Meta's Muse app, Spaces turns a request like a gift list, a word game or a DIY plan into a custom screen you keep editing by chat. You choose what sits in the sidebar, hide the rest, and can schedule screens, say a weekday planner that becomes a weekend one.

Student Corner

Perception · Best Illusion of the Year · 8 Nov

Two gears, same speed, different feeling

This year's winning illusion, from the University of British Columbia, shows two gears turning at the same speed. Stare at a dot on the left gear and the right one seems faster, because side vision is more sensitive to motion. Motion designers: where the eye rests changes what speed feels like.

Studio profile · Figur · 4 Nov

"If it's fiction, it's not a Fig"

Stockholm studio Figur designs for sportswear brands like Hoka and Unna, and its founder runs ultras himself. Its first rule: no photoshopped case studies, so every mockup is made for real. Student lesson: put a few real printed pieces in your portfolio, not just mockups.

Design psychology term of the day

Framing effect

The same choice feels different depending on how it is worded. A paid trial called a "gift" feels free, even if it renews. That is today's lead story: framing can help people understand, or push them into something they did not choose.

Try it: write two versions of a trial button, "Start free gift" and "Free for 1 month, then ₹99/month". Ask five friends which one they would trust more.
