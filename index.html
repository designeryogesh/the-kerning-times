Lead story · Generative AI · Design tools · Canva · 20 Nov

Canva lets you switch AI off across the app

Canva has added a switch that hides its generative AI features, and a filter that limits AI content in its library. Both are free on Canva Free and Pro.

What changed. The Generative AI switch sits under Profile, then Settings, then Permissions. When it is off, AI features are hidden across Canva. You can turn them back on at any time, and your existing designs are not changed.

The library filter. The second control, under Your account and then AI Personalization, reduces AI-generated content in library search and recommendations. Canva says this brings more human-made work to the front, since its library mixes creator work with some AI-made elements.

What stays on. Assistive tools such as Background Remover keep working with AI switched off. Canva says they only edit existing content, so they do not add its AI-generated metadata, and social platforms will not label that work as AI.

The context. The change comes about two weeks after Canva's job cuts and the launch of Canva Craft, its first in-house image model. Canva says its community has used generative tools more than 38 billion times, and it will keep investing in them.

What this means for you

Learn both controls before your next brief. A client who wants human-made assets can filter the library today. If you design for yourself, you can switch AI off and keep the editing tools you rely on, such as background removal.

Ideogram 4.6 edits one detail and keeps the rest

Ideogram 4.6 renders natively at 4K and is built for step-by-step edits. The company says the rest of the image stays stable between edits, which helps with product shots that need many variants. Google · Nano Banana 2.1 · Workspace Updates · Nov

Google Pics moves to Nano Banana 2.1

Google Pics now runs on Nano Banana 2.1 for generation and editing. Google says it brings higher visual quality, stronger edit fidelity and sharper text. Pics also works inside Google Docs and Slides, so check any text in an image before you share the file.

Etsy · AI training terms · Cartoon Brew · 18 Nov

Artists leave Etsy over new AI training terms

From 16 December, Etsy's updated terms let it and its partners use seller content to train AI systems. Etsy says it does not train models to copy artists' work, but the terms are vague. Many artists are closing their shops before the holidays.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Winter Open-Mic Ticket

Today's brief: a 3:1 event ticket for an invented comedy night, KATTA CHOWK OPEN MIC, on 6 December. It tests a torn stub, exact Hindi and English text, a fixed count of stars and a two-colour print. No people, no real venue or brands.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: make a ticket for your own college fest. Give the stub one clever detail, such as a seat code or a joke. Ask three classmates to find the date in three seconds, and note who is fastest.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 3:1 horizontal event ticket on warm cream card, with a torn perforated stub on the right. Left panel: headline KATTA CHOWK OPEN MIC in bold charcoal sans-serif, the Hindi line हंसो, फिर सोचो in charcoal Devanagari beneath it, and the date line 6 DEC · 8 PM. Three small stars in a row in deep red. Stub panel: the number 07 and the words ADMIT ONE in deep red. Flat print texture, two colours only, charcoal and deep red on cream. No people, no faces, no real venue names, no brand logos, no QR code.

Why it works: exact text and a fixed count of stars are easy to score, and the two-colour rule shows whether the model keeps its limits.

A student's logo marks 75 years of ties

Akhil Cleatus, a Masters student at NID Bengaluru, designed the logo for 75 years of India–Japan relations in 2027. A committee from both governments picked it from more than 850 entries. It weaves cultural elements of both countries into the numbers 75, in saffron, green, red and navy.

Fake cigarette packs mock AI hype

Max Cameron made packs that copy a real cigarette pack closely, with a fictional AI-cancer brand. The warnings read 'Keep smoking, AI will save you', a joke about a Palantir executive's claim that AI would cure cancer. He wanted it 'straight and real', so the joke lands on a second look.

Design psychology term of the day

Goal gradient effect

People speed up as they get closer to a goal. A progress bar that already shows some progress gets more action than one that starts empty.

Try it: build a five-step sign-up flow with a visible progress bar, then note where users slow down or drop out.
