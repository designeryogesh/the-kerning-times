Lead story · Generative AI · Tools · 22 Oct

Canva builds its own AI image model, trained on templates

Canva has launched Craft, its first in-house image model. Most image models learn mainly from photos. Craft learned from millions of human-made templates, so it plans the layout first and draws second, and its images can be split into editable layers inside Canva.

How it works. A planning model first writes a composition plan: which elements appear, where they sit, how big they are, what the text says and what matters most. A second model then renders the picture. Because the plan is separate, one element can be changed without redrawing the whole image.

What you get. Images up to 2K in more than 30 aspect ratios, text in 55 languages and prompts in any language. With Magic Layers the result becomes editable design elements instead of a flat file. It is live in Canva and open to developers through a public API; faster "Craft-fast" and "Craft-ultrafast" versions are planned.

How good is it. On the crowd-voted DesignArena board it debuted fourth overall, behind three OpenAI models. It ranked first for portraits and for landscapes, second for abstract patterns and fourth for products, its weakest area. Canva admits fine print and technical drawings need work, and reference images are not supported yet.

Why Canva did it. Owning the model lets Canva control the cost of every image instead of paying other AI labs. During training, when automated scores and designer opinions disagreed, Canva says it "sided with the designer".

What this means for you

AI drafts will increasingly arrive already "laid out", with hierarchy baked in. Your value moves to the brief and the edit: deciding what goes where, fixing the type, checking the small text. Try one social post in Craft, split it into layers, and note how much you still had to rebuild by hand. That list is your skill.

AI Desk

Image editing · 26 Oct

Krea 3 edits product shots in under eight seconds

Krea's new model works on existing images: edit only a selected area, place items with bounding boxes, add up to six reference images, or train a LoRA on one product or style. Krea says a 2K edit costs $0.06. It is aimed at e-commerce and ad teams, for jobs like poster localisation and colour variants.

Microsoft · 27 Oct

MAI Image 2.7 can change the text inside a picture

Microsoft's new editing model restyles or relights a whole scene, swaps text and symbols so they still look part of the image, and restores old assets. It debuted third on Arena's editing board. It is a preview on MAI Playground; text-to-image is "coming soon".

World models · 27 Oct

Genie 4 builds worlds you can steer, with sound

Google DeepMind's Genie 4 simulates interactive worlds in real time and generates their audio too: ambient sound, music and dialogue. Type to move the camera, change the weather or add characters. Only trusted testers have it now; AI Ultra users and Gemini API partners come next.

Marketplaces · New rules

Etsy now wants one real, unedited photo per listing

Every Etsy listing must show at least one unedited photo of the actual item on a real background. AI can no longer make the patterns, recipes or instructions sold to buyers, and bot-made listings are banned. AI art stays allowed if it is clearly labelled.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Diwali Open-House Invite

Diwali is ten days away and studios are sending invites. Today's brief is an invitation card: a 5:7 portrait e-invite for "STUDIO KESAR", an invented design studio in Pune, hosting a Diwali open house. It tests three things AI still finds hard: Devanagari spelling, exact object counts and symmetry.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: keep only the AI background, toran and diyas. Reset all three text lines yourself in Figma with a real Devanagari typeface (such as Mukta or Tiro Devanagari Hindi) and one clean sans. Put both versions side by side at 100% zoom and list three differences.

Version 1 · Any tool
Portrait 5:7 Diwali open-house invitation card for "STUDIO KESAR", an invented design studio in Pune. Deep indigo handmade-paper background. Along the top edge: a symmetrical toran of marigolds with exactly 9 mango leaves. In the centre: "शुभ दीपावली" in elegant gold Devanagari, below it "STUDIO KESAR · DIWALI OPEN HOUSE" in small spaced white capitals, then "SAT 7 NOV · 6 PM". Bottom third: exactly 5 lit clay diyas in one straight row. Plenty of empty space. No people, no logos, no other text.

Why it works: every text line has its own slot and every object has a number, so you can check each claim in ten seconds instead of judging "does it look nice".

Graphic Design

Packaging · 26 Oct

Sixty cider labels that replay one tennis point

Vienna studio Hono made labels for Temper's pear cider that animate the last point of the 2008 Wimbledon final. There are 60 bottles and 60 labels, one frame each; line them up and they play like a flip book. One Risograph base was printed, then each label was finished and numbered by hand. The ball is punched out, so the yellow cider becomes the ball.

Opinion · 27 Oct

Is football branding the life out of itself?

In an It's Nice That column, creative director Will Knight says club rebrands now follow corporate logic: simpler crests, smoother shapes, built for screens. Each change looks sensible alone, but together they make clubs competent and anonymous. He praises Ajax for returning to its old crest and Arsenal's Rocastle Gothic typeface by David Rudnick, and asks clubs what they can make more of, not what they can simplify.

Process · 26 Oct

Bráulio Amado turns mistakes into a method

The New York-based designer, known for his Good Room club posters, finds ideas in worn street surfaces and thrown-away objects. He sets limits, like making a poster from a few materials in two hours, and repeats happy accidents on purpose. His advice: let the idea choose the style, and show clients the thinking behind unexpected options.

UI / UX

Apple · 28 Oct

The Mac finally gets a touchscreen

Apple's new MacBook Pro has a tandem-OLED screen you can touch, 2,000 nits peak brightness and a Dynamic Island in place of the notch, holding Spotlight, Live Activities and Siri. macOS 27 adds touch tweaks. Apple posted "Design for Touch on Mac" videos, live labs on 18–19 Nov, and PSD/PNG mockups on its Design Resources page. From $2,899.

Google · 26 Oct

Google Photos lets you arrange your own Collections tab

The fixed 2×2 grid is gone. A Pinned row (Favorites, Screenshots and Trash by default) sits on top, followed by carousels such as Albums, People & pets and Documents that you can hide or reorder. Everything else goes into an "Other collections" grid with a Reorder button. Rolling out on Android 7.97 and iOS 7.98.

Web design · 27 Oct

An agency website built to put off the wrong clients

Denver's Bound Agency, with studio Wunder Werkz, made a scrapbook-style site: handwritten logo, wobbly doodle icons, lilac background, tarot cards to pull and a cake you can build and share. There is no About page and no services list. The founder says the revamp "wasn't about lead-gen"; it works as a vibe check for future clients.

Design for Bharat 2027: three days left

The Youth Affairs ministry's national product-design challenge, with SPA New Delhi as knowledge partner, is free for Indian citizens aged 18–29. The Open Challenge is for enrolled students across seven themes, from smart farming to next-gen handicrafts; the Graduation Project Challenge is for 2025–26 design graduates. Teams of up to three. National showcase in New Delhi, 10–12 Jan.

Careers · 27 Oct

Many skills is not a weakness

At It's Nice That's Nicer Tuesdays in LA, ilovecreatives founder Jennifer Puno, who runs a digital trade school and a design studio, said creatives don't have to pick one discipline for life. Her line: "You're not scattered, you're multidimensional." If you juggle illustration, UI and motion, build a portfolio that shows how they connect.

Design psychology term of the day

IKEA effect

We value things more when we have put effort into making or arranging them ourselves. It is why a hand-numbered cider label feels precious, and why letting people pin and reorder their own Collections can make an app feel like theirs.

Try it: in your next app or brand project, give users one small thing to set up themselves (a pinned row, a colour, a name), then test whether they return to it more often.
