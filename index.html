Lead story · Typography · Design Compass · 18 Nov

The NYT's 175 is built from its own masthead

To mark 175 years, The New York Times and XYZ Type drew the numbers 1, 7 and 5 from the letters of the paper's own title.

The borrowing. The serif on the 1 comes from the h in the masthead. The flicked end of the 7 follows the leg of the k, and the curve of the 5 is taken from the s. When the number sits next to the title, the strokes look as if they come from one brush.

The redraw. The first try used NYT Fraktur, the blackletter drawn by Ed Benguiat. It looked flat beside the masthead, so the team tested angular and extended forms, then cut back the ornament and kept one feature from the title. The numbers first appeared at the end of a New Year's Eve video by motion designer Kiefer Keegan, and later on pages and at exhibitions.

What this means for you

Before you draw a new anniversary or event mark, look at the client's own letters first. If the new mark looks flat next to the logo, it is not finished. Redraw it until the two feel like one brush.

The Winter Bakery Sticker

Today's brief: a 1:1 round sticker for an invented winter bakery, NUKKAD BAKE CO. It tests a name that follows a circle, an exact Hindi line, a small object count and flat print. No people, hands or festival symbols.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: make a round sticker for a snack you like, with an invented name and three colours. Print it at 5 cm and read it from arm's length. Which word do you read first?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 1:1 round sticker label for an invented bakery called NUKKAD BAKE CO. Flat print style on a cream background with a thin deep-red circular border. The name NUKKAD BAKE CO. runs along the top arc in white capitals on the red band. In the centre, one illustrated nankhatai biscuit with three crumbs, and one thin wisp of steam from a cup of chai beside it. Below the biscuit, the Hindi line "ठंड में गरमागरम" in deep red, and small Latin text "Winter Batch · Fresh Daily" at the bottom. Three colours only. No people, no hands, no logos, no festival symbols.

Why it works: an arched name, an exact Hindi line and a small object count are easy to score, and the three-colour rule shows whether the model follows limits.

Brand identity · Crystal Ski · Landor · Design Compass · 18 Nov

A snowflake from a lowercase y

British ski travel brand Crystal Ski has a new identity by Landor. The split stroke of the lowercase y became a six-fold snowflake that frames photos. The wordmark's stroke ends are cut at an angle and the i dot is a rhombus. One cut from your own letterform can carry the whole system.

Typeface · PoW Dumbria · Design Compass · 17 Nov

Dumbria brings back concave stroke ends

Type designer Reymund Schröder rebuilt a 1902 woodblock sample book from Sachs & Co. Some characters were missing, so he reinterpreted them. Dumbria comes in five weights, with concave stroke ends on letters such as A and N. The foundry suggests large-text posters and book covers.

National brand · Singapore · Anak · Design Compass · 17 Nov

Singapore's red dot becomes a sphere

Singapore's new national brand by Anak turns the small red dot into a glowing sphere. The slogan "When you're ready" asks companies whether they are ready to start, invest and grow there. Viktor and DM Sans carry the type, and one sphere motif changes its content from ad to ad.

Instagram tests a separate DM app

Meta is testing Direct, a standalone messaging app that opens on the Inbox and drops the Feed and Reels tabs. A search bar sits at the top. Chats can be drawn on and given themes. It is in test in Turkey and Thailand. Give a feature its own app and its first screen must answer one question.

Google Maps · Ask Maps · Design Compass · 19 Nov

Numbered pins show the trip order

Ask Maps can now build a travel itinerary. Stops appear as numbered pins in visiting order, next to a daily list. Tapping a stop opens directions, and the plan can be exported to Google Docs. When a list and a map show the same thing, link them with one shared number.

Android · Google Play · 9to5Google · 18 Nov

Foldable apps must fill the screen by 2027

From April 2027, Play Store listings for foldables must meet Tier 3 of Google's adaptive guidelines. Apps may not letterbox, must fill the screen and must keep scroll position and text input through folding and rotation. They also need multi-window support.

Design psychology term of the day

Serial position effect

People remember the first and last items in a list best, and the middle fades. Put your most important choice at the start or end of a menu, a nav bar or a price table, not in the middle.

Try it: count the items in your phone's bottom bar or a shop's price list. Which item do you remember first, and is it the one you use most?
