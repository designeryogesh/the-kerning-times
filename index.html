Lead story · Brand identity · Design Compass · 17 Nov

Makers Mark turns its red wax into a brand system

The Kentucky bourbon brand and Turner Duckworth London have built a new identity from the red wax that runs down every bottle neck.

The wax. Each bottle is still lifted and dipped by hand. As the wax sets, it leaves marks of different lengths and shapes, so no two bottles look the same. The team took patterns from the distillery at Star Hill Farm, in Loreto, Kentucky, including wheat stalks, oak tree rings and native plants, and pressed them into the wax.

The type. The custom typeface is based on the handwriting of co-founder Marge Samuels. She and her husband Bill set up the brand in 1953 and designed the bottle, the label and the red wax seal. The new website went live on the day of the announcement, and the same marks now carry the brand's message.

What this means for you

Before you design a new mark, look at how the product is made. Makers Mark did not invent a symbol. It found its pattern in the process and in a founder's handwriting. Ask what your client already does by hand, then build the system from that.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Chai Stop Platform Sign

Today's brief: a 3:2 platform sign for an invented tea stall, CHAI STOP, at platform 4. It tests arrow direction, an exact Hindi line, a fixed count of pictograms and enamel-plate print. No people, no real railway logos or livery.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: design a directional sign for your college canteen with three pictograms. Stand 5 m away and note which pictogram you read first. Is it the one you most need?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 3:2 landscape platform sign on a cream enamel plate with a thin deep-red border. On the left, a deep-red arrow points right beside the text चाय स्टॉप in large deep-red Devanagari. Below the text, three simple pictograms in a row: a cup of chai, a bench and an exit door, each in deep red outline. Flat print texture, two colours only, cream and deep red. No people, no faces, no railway logos, no real station names.

Why it works: an arrow, an exact Hindi line and a fixed pictogram count are easy to score, and the two-colour rule shows whether the model keeps limits.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
platform sign, 3:2, cream enamel plate, thin deep-red border, large deep-red arrow pointing right beside Devanagari text चाय स्टॉप, three pictograms in a row: cup of chai, bench, exit door, flat screen print, two colours only, no people, no railway logos --ar 3:2 --style raw

V8 tip: Midjourney may bend the Devanagari matras. Set the Hindi line in a font after generation, and count the pictograms by eye.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a 3:2 platform sign on a cream enamel plate with a thin deep-red border. Three simple pictograms in a row in the lower third: a cup of chai, a bench and an exit door, outlined in deep red. No text yet. No people.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep everything the same. Add a large deep-red arrow pointing right on the left side, and the Devanagari line चाय स्टॉप in deep red beside it. Do not change the pictograms or the border.

What you are testing: whether step 2 adds the arrow and the Hindi line without redrawing the three pictograms.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 3:2 platform sign for the invented tea stall CHAI STOP.

Layout:
1. Background: cream enamel plate, full frame
2. Border: one thin deep-red line near the edge
3. Left: one large deep-red arrow pointing right
4. Beside the arrow: चाय स्टॉप in deep red, Devanagari spelled exactly
5. Lower third: three pictograms in a row, a cup of chai, a bench, an exit door, outlined in deep red

Style: flat screen print, two colours only. No people, no faces, no railway logos, no station names.

Tip: if the Hindi line breaks, reply "fix only the Devanagari line; keep everything else the same".

Design psychology term of the day

Zeigarnik effect

People remember unfinished tasks better than finished ones. A progress bar or a "continue" card brings users back because the task is still open in their head.

Try it: start a sketch, stop halfway, then note what you think about on the way back to it.
