Lead story · Product design · Tesla · 6 Nov

Tesla winds down the Cybertruck

Elon Musk says Cybertruck production in Texas will "wind down" from mid-2027 to make room for the Cybercab robotaxi. Seven years after its shock reveal, the boldest-looking vehicle of the decade is being quietly parked.

The news. Dezeen reports that Musk announced the change on X on Friday. It is not clear if the truck will be fully discontinued, and the Texas Gigafactory is the only plant known to build it. Musk replied that the existing fleet "will be around for a long time".

The numbers. At a 2023 shareholders' meeting Musk predicted 250,000 to 500,000 sales a year. Kelley Blue Book estimates Tesla sold 20,237 in the US in 2025, down from 38,965 in 2024. Many trucks were recalled in 2024 over a faulty accelerator pedal.

What went wrong. Creative Bloq lists the problems: windows that shattered at the 2019 launch demo, delays, a higher price than promised, loose panels, and a heavy angular body that failed European pedestrian-safety rules. Its stainless steel also brought manufacturing and finishing headaches.

The branding. Creative Bloq also argues the aggressive type and identity pushed the truck further into concept-car territory, when the silhouette was already loud enough. Its verdict: good product design must turn heads, work, and make sense for the buyer. The Cybertruck struggled on all three.

What this means for you

Being different gets attention; being useful keeps customers. When you pitch a bold concept, test it against everyday use: can it be produced, can people read it, does it survive real conditions? And keep one part calm: if the shape shouts, let the type and logo speak quietly.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Diwali Eve Hoarding

Tomorrow is Diwali, and tonight every sweet shop is busy. Today's brief is outdoor advertising: a wide roadside hoarding for an invented mithai shop that a driver can read in three seconds. Hoardings punish clutter, and AI tools love clutter.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: build the hoarding in Illustrator at 48 × 16 ft (scale 1:20). Print it at A4, stick it on a wall 5 metres away and check: can you read the headline and the shop name in three seconds?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 3:1 roadside hoarding for GHEE & GUR, an invented Indian sweet shop, for Diwali eve. Deep indigo night background with a soft warm glow on the right. On the right third: one open cardboard mithai box holding exactly nine round besan laddoos in a 3 × 3 grid, lit warmly from above. Left two-thirds: the headline "Mithai till midnight." in a heavy condensed sans, cream colour. Under it, smaller: "मीठी दिवाली". Bottom left: the shop name "GHEE & GUR" once, with "Open till 12 am, 7 Nov". Flat, high-contrast poster design, lots of empty space, no other text.

Why it works: it divides the canvas into clear zones, counts the laddoos and lists every word, so you can see exactly where the tool adds clutter.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
outdoor billboard design, deep indigo night background, one open cardboard sweet box with nine round besan laddoos in a neat 3 by 3 grid on the right, warm golden light from above, large empty space on the left for a headline, bold flat poster style, high contrast, minimal --ar 3:1 --style raw --no fireworks, people, text

V8 tip: keep the left side empty with --no text, then set the headline, Devanagari line and logo yourself in Illustrator. Billboard type should never be left to chance.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a 3:1 wide billboard image: deep indigo night background, one open cardboard box with exactly nine round besan laddoos in a 3 × 3 grid on the right third, warm light from above, the left two-thirds empty.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the box, laddoos and light exactly the same. On the empty left, add "Mithai till midnight." in a heavy cream condensed sans, "मीठी दिवाली" below it, and "GHEE & GUR · Open till 12 am, 7 Nov" small at the bottom left.

What you are testing: whether step 2 adds or melts any laddoos, and whether the Hindi line stays correct when English text sits next to it.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 3:1 billboard for "GHEE & GUR", an invented sweet shop.

Layout:
1. Background: deep indigo night
2. Right third: one open box, 9 laddoos, 3 × 3
3. Left: "Mithai till midnight." (huge, cream)
4. Below it: "मीठी दिवाली" (Devanagari)
5. Bottom left: "GHEE & GUR"
6. Next to it: "Open till 12 am, 7 Nov"
7. Warm light only on the box

No fireworks, deities, people or other text.

Tip: if the headline is too small, reply "make the headline fill half the height, change nothing else". Then test the result as a 200-pixel thumbnail.

Typeface · Paper · 5 Nov

A free monospace font built for both code and reading

Paper Mono has eight weights (100 to 800) as a variable font, 800 glyphs and 100+ languages. Its "duospace" option makes some letters wider or narrower so long text reads better, and it adds coding ligatures, a slashed zero and 128 box-drawing characters for tables and progress bars.

Identity · Crystal · 5 Nov

A music agency's logo that reacts to each track

Portland music agency Crystal, founded in 2014, worked with studio Forum to reposition itself as a curator, not just a supplier. The wordmark responds to the sound elements of each track, while SourceType's serif Magister and a fixed grid keep everything consistent.

AI UX · Nielsen Norman Group · 6 Nov

Learn the word "harness" before you design an AI agent

NN/g defines an agent harness as the software around an AI model that runs its loop: it carries out tool requests, returns results, and controls what the model sees and may do. Two assistants on the same model can do very different things because of their harness.

Illustration · Creative Bloq · 6 Nov

Try a week without colour

Four illustrators explain why they work in black and white. Sarah Koudelka-Mason likes pencils because they go anywhere; Joe Vriens loved bare comic line work; Isabella Mazzanti draws on a long tradition from Gustave Doré's engravings to manga. A limit can become a style.

Design psychology term of the day

Hick's law

The more choices people see, the longer they take to decide. Each extra option adds a little time, and it adds up fast in menus, settings and hoardings. That is why WhatsApp offers just four privacy options and a good billboard has one message.

Try it: take a menu with 12 items, group it into 3 sections of 4, and time five friends finding one item in each version.
