Lead story · Video tools · Instagram · 5 Nov

Instagram's Edits app moves to the Mac

Instagram has released Edits, its free video editor, as a desktop app for Mac. It brings a full timeline, your own fonts and colour LUTs, 4K exports, and a door for AI agents to cut videos for you.

What you get. MacRumors reports a multi-track timeline, keyframes for transitions and audio waveforms for precise cuts. Unite.AI adds colour wheels and curves for grading. You can import LUTs and font files, use Instagram's licensed and royalty-free music, and get unlimited automatic captions.

Built for every platform. Projects can use several vertical and horizontal aspect ratios, export in 4K with no watermark, or publish straight to Instagram without downloading and re-uploading. Keyboard shortcuts can be carried over from another editor, and text and audio styles saved on your phone show up on desktop.

AI agents can edit too. Every tool in the app is open to AI agents through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). According to Unite.AI, citing The Verge, a creator can ask Meta's Muse or another agent to build a rough cut from a video in the Downloads folder, then open that project and finish it by hand.

The limits. It needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) and an Instagram login. There is no Windows or Linux version, and you cannot yet edit the same project on phone and Mac. Meta says cross-device editing is coming, with weekly updates to the desktop app.

What this means for you

If you design Reels for brands, set up a brand kit now: the font files, one LUT and one caption style that every video must use. When an AI agent can make the rough cut, your value is in the final edit: the pacing, the type and the colour that make a brand feel like itself.

Circle the spot you want changed

Beyond yesterday's GIF news, afaqs reports that Images 2.6 lets API users circle an area of an image to guide an edit, set frame counts and playback speed, and generate up to 50% faster. Developers also get SVG output plus two new modes: one for speed, one for fine detail.

Dior's "hand-drawn" ad faces AI questions

Viewers spotted warped lettering and odd fingers in Dior's animated holiday film. The behind-the-scenes clip shows only sketches, and the credits do not mention AI. Creative Bloq says AI use is still unconfirmed, but the silence itself is what made audiences angry. Lesson: say what tools you used.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Dhanteras Kadha Tin

Dhanteras is about health as much as wealth, so today's brief is packaging for a gift people actually use: a round tin of winter kadha mix for TULSI & TIN, an invented brand. Curved labels are hard for AI, because the text must bend with the tin.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: draw the flat label in Illustrator as a 250 × 90 mm wrap, with a 3 mm bleed and the ingredients panel set in a real Devanagari and Latin type pair. Then wrap it onto a tin mockup and compare it with the AI version.

Version 1 · Any tool
Square 1:1 packaging photo for TULSI & TIN, an invented Indian wellness brand, for Dhanteras gifting. One round brushed-steel tin, 10 cm tall, with a matte turmeric-yellow paper label wrapped around it. On the label: "TULSI & TIN" in a bold serif, "सेहत ही धन" in Devanagari below, and "Winter Kadha · 100 g" in small caps. A row of exactly five flat line icons: tulsi leaf, ginger root, cinnamon stick, black pepper, turmeric. Tin on a terracotta surface, one soft window light from the left. No deities, coins, medical claims or other logos.

Why it works: it fixes the tin's material and size, writes every word on the label, and counts the icons, so each line of the checklist can be scored.

Graphic Design

Logo · Gillette · 5 Nov

Gillette confirms the blade hidden in its logo

Gillette's social team confirmed on Instagram what fans long suspected: a razor blade sits inside the wordmark. One designer in the comments says the double "ll" is the blade; another asks why it lacks a blade's notches. Hidden ideas work best when, like this one, they come from the product itself.

Poster · Montreux Jazz Festival · 5 Nov

Two artists share one festival poster again

For only the second time since Andy Warhol and Keith Haring in 1986, the festival paired two artists: French illustrator Malika Favre and Sri Lankan artist Kumkum Fernando. Her 2017 poster was minimal; this one is maximalist, mixing South Asian traditions and retro-futurism. It is also being built as a vinyl cabinet.

Campaign identity · Amsterdam Museum Night · 5 Nov

A museum night designed for getting lost

Rotterdam studio From Form, in its fifth year on the job, drew on surrealism and 17th-century dioramas: floating keys, clocks and torches, with animation built from photographs. Faces borrow the long necks of old paintings. The aim is to make visitors wander the 65+ museums without a plan.

UI / UX

Android · WhatsApp · 5 Nov

Long-press to peek at a WhatsApp chat

WhatsApp beta 2.26.44.11 for Android shows the latest messages when you long-press a chat, with quick actions below: mark as read, pin, archive, delete, and more. You can read without sending read receipts. iPhone users already have this, so Android is catching up to a familiar pattern.

Experiment · YouTube · 5 Nov

YouTube's half-dark screen is a test, not a bug

Some users saw the watch page split in two: the player and comments in Ambient mode, the recommendations below in light or dark, depending on their setting. YouTube told Android Authority it is a small test on Android and iOS to make watching more immersive. Many first reported it as a bug, which is useful feedback in itself.

Accessibility · Smashing Magazine · 5 Nov

AI chat screens must work for everyone

Designer Eric Bailey says full-screen AI chats like ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are still web interfaces under the same accessibility rules. People do two things: type and send, then read and act. Watch for "visual bias": design for screen readers and keyboards too, not just for the eye.

Student Corner

Typography · Suitcase Type Foundry · 5 Nov

Choose a font for its footnotes, not its mockup

Tomáš Brousil compares type mockups to a burger ad: everything faces the camera. His advice: test a family at every level, from headline to caption; check figures, currency symbols and language support; remember that a good retail font is often enough. Common mistakes: ignoring optical sizes, OpenType features and the licence.

Career · Tatiana Egoshina · 5 Nov

From one-colour posters to a mountain-sized canvas

The Milan-based designer learnt in Moscow to work with black, white and one extra colour, then worked at Strelka Institute and Readymag. For a recent projection piece on cliffs in southern Italy, she spent a week listening to village elders' stories, "things you simply cannot Google". It won first prize.

Design psychology term of the day

Aesthetic–usability effect

People tend to believe a good-looking design is easier to use, and they forgive its small flaws. That helps, but it can also hide real problems in testing. YouTube's ambient watch page looks more immersive, yet many users still thought it was broken.

Try it: make a polished and a plain version of the same form with the same mistake in both. Ask five people which is easier and see who spots the mistake.
