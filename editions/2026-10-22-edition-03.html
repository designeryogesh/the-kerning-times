Lead story · Branding · Sound

Apple Music's new logo was shaped by real sound

On 21 October, Apple Music officially launched its new identity. The music-note mark was not drawn on a screen: a piece of music was played into a shallow bath of oil, and the ripples it made were filmed to become the logo. Apple calls it "just music, made visible".

How it was made. Apple worked with producer Fred again.. and his collaborator NEST Acoustics. A new composition was split into single notes and played through a transducer, a device that turns an audio signal into physical vibration. The transducer sat in moving mineral oil, and the waves, ripples and light refractions were filmed. The final mark is a recording of what the music physically did, not an illustration of it.

What changed on screen. The pink-and-white icon is gone. The new one is monochrome, close to the old note shape, with liquid-glass details at the corners, in line with Apple's Liquid Glass design language. The wordmark pairs the Apple logo with a custom bold lowercase version of SF Pro.

Sound and system. The same composition also gives Apple Music a new audio identity, so the logo and the sonic signature come from one source. Reports say the work took months of experiments, and a short film documents the process. The identity will run across marketing, product and live events.

When you will see it. The wordmark first appeared in September, in marketing for Apple Music Hall, a new live venue in London. It now shows up in Apple Music's own content and events, and 9to5Mac reports it reaches iPhones with iOS 27.2, expected next week. Apple used a similar hands-on, in-camera method for its Apple TV rebrand.

What this means for you

A logo story sells the logo. Apple's mark looks only slightly different, but "made by sound" gives everyone a reason to talk about it. On your next identity project, try making one asset by hand or with a real process: ink in water, light through glass, a stamp, a scan. Film the making, because the process clip is often the best launch post on Instagram and LinkedIn.

AI Desk

Video · 16–21 Oct

Kling 4.0 makes 30-second clips in one go

Kuaishou launched Kling 4.0 worldwide on 16 October, and it reached Higgsfield on 21 October. It makes 3 to 30 seconds of video from text, images or references, takes up to 10 keyframes and 15 references, and can edit finished clips. 10-bit HDR is "coming soon", and Kling says the model is also coming to Adobe Firefly.

Tools · 19 Oct

Higgsfield adds lighter video edits and 3D angles

Genjutsu Lite swaps motion or objects in 4–15 second clips at a lower credit cost, using up to 10 reference images. A new 3D Angles feature in Higgsfield's ChatGPT plugin turns a photo, video or text into a scene you can "reshoot" with 24–300 mm lenses. Useful for product shots from new angles.

Brand mascots · 19 Oct

A bank's AI sheep shows mascots can't be controlled

New Zealand's Kiwibank used AI to animate Buck, a green-woolled sheep mascot. The internet turned Buck into a meme and spread unofficial AI images. The bank's copyright takedowns made the story bigger, a classic Streisand effect. Creative Bloq's advice: act on harmful content, tolerate harmless fan art.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Ripple Logo Post

Inspired by today's lead. The brief is a square Instagram brand key visual for "leher", an invented Indian indie music app launching its Diwali playlists. The logo should look like it was made by sound: ripples in a brass bowl of water with diya reflections. The hard parts for AI: counted rings, counted reflections, two scripts and a clean text zone.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: keep the best AI water plate, then build the real "leher" logo yourself in Illustrator from the ripple shape. Make a 1:1 feed post, a 9:16 story and a 1920 × 1080 YouTube banner. For extra credit, film your own ripple with a phone, a speaker and a steel plate of water, and compare it with the AI version.

Version 1 · Any tool
Square 1:1 brand key visual for "leher", an invented Indian music app launching Diwali playlists. Top-down close-up of a wide brass bowl filled with still dark water. In the centre, exactly 5 clean ripple rings spread outward, as if a single sound made them. Exactly 3 small diya flames are reflected in the water near the edge. Warm gold light on deep navy shadows. Top quarter: calm dark space with the line "DIWALI PLAYLISTS ARE LIVE" in small white capitals. Bottom centre: the wordmark "leher" in clean lowercase white letters, with "लहर" smaller beside it. No hands, no people, no instruments, no other text.

Why it works: every element has a number or a fixed place, so you can score it in ten seconds. Asking for "still water" first stops the model from filling the bowl with random waves.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
top-down product photo, wide brass bowl of still dark water, exactly five concentric ripple rings from the centre, three small oil-lamp flames reflected near the rim, warm gold highlights, deep navy shadows, minimal, calm empty dark space in the top quarter, premium music brand campaign, macro detail, soft studio light --ar 1:1 --stylize 80 --v 8

V8 tip: leave all text out and add the wordmark and Devanagari in your edit. If the ring count drifts, try Thinking Mode's rerun and count again.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a square top-down photo of a wide brass bowl of still dark water. Exactly 5 clean ripple rings spread out from the centre. Exactly 3 small diya flames are reflected in the water near the rim. Warm gold light, deep navy shadows. Keep the top quarter as calm dark space. No text, no hands, no people.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the bowl, rings and reflections exactly the same. Add "DIWALI PLAYLISTS ARE LIVE" in small white capitals in the top quarter, and the wordmark "leher" in clean lowercase white letters at the bottom centre, with "लहर" smaller beside it.

What you are testing: whether adding text makes the model redraw the water. Count rings and flames after step 1 and again after step 2.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a square 1:1 Instagram post for "leher", an invented Indian music app.

Layout, top to bottom:
1. Small white capitals: DIWALI PLAYLISTS ARE LIVE (on calm dark space)
2. Centre: top-down view of a brass bowl of still dark water with exactly 5 ripple rings from the middle
3. Near the rim: exactly 3 diya flames reflected in the water
4. Bottom centre: wordmark "leher" in clean lowercase white, with "लहर" smaller beside it

Warm gold light, deep navy shadows. No hands, people, instruments or other text.

Tip: if the count is off, reply "fix only line 2: exactly 5 rings, keep everything else". Small, numbered fixes work better than a full re-prompt.

Graphic Design

Identity · Film · 21 Oct

A film company brand with chaos built in

LA studio Day Job branded To Be Formed, the production company of filmmaker Lance Oppenheim. The florid wordmark mixes thick shapes with thin calligraphic letters, inspired by off-kilter old book covers and early-2000s DVD menus. About 20 secondary wordmarks swap in and out, so the identity keeps changing, just like his films.

Publishing · Type · 22 Oct

A book set only in typefaces by women

"All Work is Women's Work", by researcher Hanka van der Voet and designer Beau Bertens, recovers the story of an early Amsterdam seamstresses' union and its newspaper. Every typeface is by a woman designer: TorreFarfan, Wondertype and CCM Coda. Ornaments come from leftist papers of that era, and the book even includes stickers and a map.

Publishing · Hands-on · 20 Oct

A book you tear apart to make more books

Designer Carolina Incerti's "A Book for Making Books" holds eight templates, from stitched books to one-page zines, that readers cut, tear and fold. Members of her book club tested them. The playground-style primary colours and the open-source typeface CirrusCumulus keep it friendly, not fussy.

UI / UX

CSS · 20 Oct

CSS can now add random imperfection

The new CSS random() function gives each element a slightly different tilt, size or delay without JavaScript. Safari supports it first and Chrome is adding it in version 156. Smashing's guide warns: never randomise body text, layouts or buttons, hide split letters from screen readers, and respect reduced-motion settings.

Redesign · 16 Oct

Google Translate moves handwriting into the box

Translate on Android is getting a Material 3 redesign: a bottom navigation bar, a bigger text card, handwriting next to the mic instead of in a three-dot menu, and paste moved to the left. The trade-off is that the larger card pushes shortcuts down the screen. A clear lesson: hidden menus hide features.

Ethics · 20 Oct

Should a video call ask to "touch up" your face?

A Creative Bloq writer was asked "Touch up your appearance?" while joining Google Meet. The filters, like "dewy fresh", follow makeup looks, with no masculine-leaning options. She worries beauty could become a work standard. Before adding any appearance feature, ask who it is designed for and who it might pressure.

Student Corner

Careers · Bengaluru · 20 Oct

A designer pitched herself with a short film

After a tough year of AI anxiety, Bengaluru designer Darshita Patankar made "Design is Dead", a four-minute film shot in 8K with her brother Vikrant producing. It got nearly 3 million views on X in about a day. Creative Bloq's point: it works because of her story and personality, not the budget. Start with yours.

Free kits · Partner feature · 21 Oct

Free poster kits to practise in Affinity and Cavalry

In a brand partnership with It's Nice That, designers Melina K and Rob Farmer each made a "Free Form" poster and shared kits: textures, bird silhouettes, patterns and editable type for Affinity, plus an editable Cavalry animation. Both Canva-owned apps are free to download, so this is easy practice for students.

Design psychology term of the day

Bouba–kiki effect

Most people, across languages, match the made-up word "bouba" to a round, soft shape and "kiki" to a spiky one. Our brains link sounds and shapes. That is why a sound-made logo, like today's lead, can feel "right" before we know why.

Try it: say your client's brand name out loud. Does it sound round or sharp? Pick letterforms and shapes that match that sound.
