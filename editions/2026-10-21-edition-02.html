Lead story · Wayfinding · Signage

When staff print their own signs, the system has a gap

In almost every station of Singapore's MRT, station managers stick up A4 paper signs made in Word and printed on office inkjets. A new It's Nice That essay by writer Justin Zhuang asks a sharp question: are these design disasters, or the most honest user research a wayfinding team could get?

What the signs say. Most are simple: which exit goes where, hold the handrail, use the lift for bulky bags, answers to common questions. They use all caps, bold, underlines and colour, sometimes squashed to fit. Ugly, yes. But each one marks a spot where real people got confused.

Three stations, three lessons. Bencoolen is Singapore's deepest station at 43 metres, with at least six escalators, so staff put a sign at every escalator end to confirm the route. At Stevens, a stacked interchange, so many people tapped out by mistake that staff added an extra-large "Do Not Tap Out" sign. At Boon Keng, a colourful sign lists old landmarks and a bus route the official maps leave out.

Why it happens. The network grew from about 6 km and two lines to more than 140 stations on six lines, heading for about 360 km by the early 2030s. The official signage was redesigned in 2001 and again in 2020, but older lines still mix both systems. Samuel Lim, who led the 2020 redesign, says he regrets not extending information points to the platforms.

The flip side. Too many temporary notices can stop people from learning the real system, and a station starts to feel like it is always giving orders. Standard templates bring order, but no template can predict every situation.

What this means for you

Every taped-up note, sticky label or "click here" arrow drawn on a screenshot is free feedback about your design. Before your next signage, app or website project, go and look for the workarounds people already use. Photograph them, list the question each one answers, and design for those questions first.

AI Desk

Copyright · 14 Oct

Midjourney copied famous characters in 37% of tests

VN.ai, a startup that stress-tests AI tools for copyright, tested about 15 popular models in its first quarterly report. Midjourney did worst, producing look-alikes of characters such as Elsa and the Joker. The report says it has not improved much since Disney and NBCUniversal sued it 18 months ago. Check every AI image before it goes to a client.

Research · 20 Oct

A small image model that skips language training

Stanford researchers, including Fei-Fei Li, released Halcyon, a family of text-to-image models trained from scratch on a 100-million-image dataset, with no language pre-training. The 1.6-billion-parameter version beat a bigger rival with 275 times less compute. The lesson: smarter training can matter more than size.

Speed · 15 Oct

Photoshop rebuilt for faster GPU work

Adobe and NVIDIA say Photoshop, Premiere and Substance 3D are tuned for NVIDIA's new RTX Spark chip, with up to 2× faster AI processing and effects. Photoshop now uses GPU compositing, which speeds up the Remove Tool and live effects like Gaussian Blur. More will be shown at Adobe MAX, 10–12 November.

Tools · 12–15 Oct

Runway's MCP adds images, sound and cut-outs

Paid Runway users can now call Seedream 5.0 Flash for 1K or 2K images from text or up to 10 reference images, Mirelo SFX 2.0 for sound effects on a separate track, and BiRefNet v2 for automatic background removal, handy for product shots.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Diwali Thriller Poster

Diwali is the biggest release window for Indian cinema. Today's brief is film key art: a teaser poster for "KHIDKI", an invented Hindi crime thriller set in a Mumbai chawl. The hard parts for AI: a bilingual title, a counted grid of lit windows, one faceless silhouette and a clean space for the billing block.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: keep the best AI building as a background plate, then design the title treatment yourself in Illustrator, with your own cut, crack or light effect on the letters. Add a real billing block with invented credits, set it on a 27 × 40 inch poster, and make 4:5 Instagram and 9:16 story versions. Post all three side by side with the AI original.

Version 1 · Any tool
Vertical teaser poster for "KHIDKI", an invented Hindi crime thriller releasing at Diwali. Night view, straight on, of an old four-storey Mumbai chawl: a grid of 20 windows, 5 across and 4 down. Exactly 7 windows glow warm orange with diya light; the other 13 are dark. In one lit window on the third floor, a single black silhouette of a person, no face visible. Rain-wet street, deep blue shadows. Top: the title "KHIDKI" in large cracked white capitals, with "खिडकी" smaller below it. Above the empty bottom fifth: "IN CINEMAS THIS DIWALI". Leave the bottom fifth dark and empty. No other text.

Why it works: it gives the grid size and the exact count, so the "7 of 20" test is easy to check. Asking for empty space instead of credits avoids the gibberish small print most models invent.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
film teaser poster, night, straight-on view of an old four-storey Mumbai chawl building, grid of 20 windows five across four down, exactly seven windows glowing warm orange with oil-lamp light, the rest dark, one lone black silhouette in a third-floor window, rain-wet street, deep blue shadows, title text "KHIDKI" in cracked white capitals at the top, dark empty space at the bottom, cinematic, high contrast --ar 2:3 --stylize 100 --v 8

V8 tip: leave the Devanagari and the release line out here and add them in your edit; asking for two scripts at once usually breaks both. Low stylize keeps the window count closer to seven.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a vertical 2:3 night photo, straight on, of an old four-storey Mumbai chawl with 20 windows, 5 across and 4 down. Exactly 7 windows glow warm orange with diya light; 13 are dark. One black silhouette of a person in a third-floor lit window, face not visible. Rain-wet street, deep blue shadows. Keep the top quarter and bottom fifth as dark sky and street. No text.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the building, lights and silhouette exactly the same. Add "KHIDKI" in large cracked white capitals at the top, "खिडकी" smaller below it, and "IN CINEMAS THIS DIWALI" in small white capitals just above the empty bottom fifth.

What you are testing: whether adding text makes the model redraw the windows. Count the lit windows after step 1 and again after step 2.

Graphic Design

Sport · Type · 19 Oct

Fulham finds its typeface on its own walls

Agency Music and F37 Foundry refreshed Fulham FC's identity around a new typeface, FFC Original. It is based on hand-painted lettering found inside Craven Cottage, London's oldest football stadium. It already runs across matchday graphics, retail and the away-kit film. Good proof that a brand's best asset can be hiding in its own building.

Music · Identity · 20 Oct

A lightning bolt in milkshake flavours

Vienna's Studio Herrström rebranded Milkshake, a Stockholm label under Sony Music. The mark is a fluid lightning shape, the palette keeps the old bright colours but tunes them to milkshake flavours, and the main typeface is Gates by Context Type. Car-window stickers and road signs inspire the system, because the label's name came up on a drive.

Reading pick · Typography

A dictionary of text you cannot read

Laurenz Brunner of SourceType spent about ten years on "Dictionary of the Illegible", an A–Z of text that hides its meaning: metal band logos, comic swear symbols, emoji used as code and spellings that dodge social media filters. A useful reminder that type communicates mood even before it is read.

UI / UX

Research · 16 Oct

AI "users" agree, but think alike

NN/g asked 120 AI-generated respondents a survey question it had earlier put to 604 professionals. Both picked "UX" as the main term, but humans gave more than 50 different answers while the bots settled on about a dozen. Use synthetic users for early hunches, says NN/g, and real people for surprises and edge cases.

Interface · 17 Oct

WhatsApp tests two new chat bars

In Android beta 2.26.41.11, WhatsApp is working on a chat bar where the text box sits on top and the send button moves inside the bar. One layout keeps emoji on the right; the other puts every button on the left and leaves send alone on the right. Nothing is confirmed yet. Try sketching both and ask which is easier to use with one thumb.

Who Apple picked as Designers of Tomorrow

Apple and Design Miami opened the second Designers of Tomorrow show in Paris, on the theme of presence. Four emerging studios from Denmark, Brooklyn, Seoul and Mexico were picked. One base uses about 5,000 thin wooden veneers. The common thread: each shared a clear, documented making process. Keep a process diary for your projects too.

Design psychology term of the day

Peak–end rule

We judge an experience mostly by its most intense moment and by how it ends, not by the average of every moment. A smooth checkout with a warm thank-you screen is remembered better than a perfect form with a cold ending.

Try it: in your next app flow or event poster series, design one high point and one memorable last screen or last poster. Spend your extra effort there.
