Lead story · Tools · Affinity · 2 Nov

Free Affinity lands on iPad, built for touch

Canva has released a single Affinity app for iPad. It brings most of the free desktop software, covering photo editing, vector art and page layout, to Apple tablets, and it is free for individual users.

What's new. A year ago Canva made Affinity free on desktop and merged Photo, Designer and Publisher into one app. The old separate iPad apps kept working, but they could not open the new .af file format. The new iPad app fixes that gap, Creative Bloq reports.

Touch first. It is not a squeezed desktop. It supports Apple Pencil pressure and tilt for sketching and masking, plus Pencil Pro gestures like barrel roll, squeeze and hover. iPad-only tools include Sketch Studio for freehand drawing, sketch-to-vector, a Mask Tool and Sketch to Shapes.

One file, every job. The same .af file moves between sketch, vector, photo and layout work, with live non-destructive edits and no export step. Sketches stay editable inside the file instead of being traced again. Raw photo editing and print-ready multi-page documents are included.

The catch. You need a free Canva account. AI tools such as Generative Expand, upscaling, denoising and Decompose Layer, which splits a flat image into editable layers, need a paid Canva subscription. Last month Affinity also added more raw colour, sharpening and detail controls on desktop.

What this means for you

If you own an iPad, you now have a free, professional tool for sketching, vectors, photo retouching and layout in one place. For students who can't pay for a monthly suite, this is a real option. Try one full job on it this week: sketch a logo with the Pencil, turn it into vectors, then place it on a poster, all inside one .af file.

Google · India · 2 Nov

Dreambeans brings AI picture stories to India

Google's experimental Dreambeans app, which turns your Gmail, Photos, Calendar and YouTube activity into illustrated stories without a prompt, has launched in India, its first market outside the US. It supports 13 Indian languages, but only one at a time, so no Hinglish yet. Some stories are now animated with Veo. You choose which services to connect.

OpenAI · 2 Nov

ChatGPT Classic retires; Chat and Work merge

The old ChatGPT Classic desktop app will retire on 30 January 2027, with chat history moving to the new app. OpenAI will also start testing one combined Chat and Work screen with a small group in the coming weeks, after its president admitted the current layout was "kind of a mess". Codex stays separate.

Copyright · 2 Nov

New York Times sues Brave over AI scraping

The New York Times has sued the Brave browser company, saying it scraped the paper's articles and sold them to AI firms. The suit says Brave's index covers over 30 billion pages and publishers could not block its crawler. Three other US papers filed a similar case. Brave had not replied at the time of reporting.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Diwali Book Cover

Diwali week is when many of us finally buy that novel. Today's brief is a book cover for "Ek Diya, Do Shehar" (One Lamp, Two Cities), an invented Hindi-English novel by an invented author, Meera Joshi, from an invented publisher, Panna Books. Covers are hard for AI: exact title text, a clean author line and one simple symbol.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: make the cover in AI, then set all the type yourself in Affinity or Figma. Design the spine and back cover too, using the same grid. A cover only works if it still reads at thumbnail size, so check it at 120 px wide.

Version 1 · Any tool
Front cover of a paperback novel, portrait 2:3. Deep indigo night background. In the centre, exactly one small clay diya with one warm flame. A thin line of golden light runs from the diya to both edges: on the left it traces a dense city skyline with tall buildings, on the right it traces soft hills. Flat, minimal illustration with paper grain. Top: "EK DIYA, DO SHEHAR" in large cream serif capitals. Below it: "एक दिया, दो शहर". Bottom: "MEERA JOSHI". Small at the very bottom: "PANNA BOOKS". No people, no faces, no other text.

Why it works: one symbol, one line, four pieces of text. Every check has a single right answer you can see at a glance.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
minimal book cover illustration, one clay diya with a single flame at the centre, thin golden line of light flowing left into a dense city skyline and right into soft rolling hills, deep indigo night, cream accents, risograph paper grain, generous empty space at top and bottom for title, no text, no people --ar 2:3 --style raw --stylize 150 --no letters, faces

V8 tip: keep text out of Midjourney. Leave clean space top and bottom, then set the title, Devanagari line and author yourself.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a 2:3 minimal book cover illustration. Deep indigo night. Exactly one clay diya with one flame in the centre. A thin golden line of light runs left into a city skyline and right into soft hills. Leave empty space at the top and bottom. No text, no people.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the picture exactly the same. Add "EK DIYA, DO SHEHAR" in cream serif capitals at the top, "एक दिया, दो शहर" below it, "MEERA JOSHI" at the bottom and a small "PANNA BOOKS" under that.

What you are testing: whether adding text in step 2 adds a second diya, breaks the light line or misspells the Devanagari.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 2:3 front cover for "Ek Diya, Do Shehar", an invented novel.

Layout, top to bottom:
1. "EK DIYA, DO SHEHAR" in large cream serif capitals
2. "एक दिया, दो शहर" in Devanagari
3. Centre: one clay diya, one flame; a golden line of light to a city skyline (left) and hills (right)
4. "MEERA JOSHI"
5. Small: "PANNA BOOKS"

Deep indigo background. No people, no quotes, no other text.

Tip: if the Devanagari breaks, reply "fix only the Hindi line, keep everything else". Then ask for a flat spine strip to match.

Rebrand · 2 Nov

How&How turns itself up to HOW+HOW

Six years after launch, the London and LA studio has rebranded itself around its own advice, "turn up your truth". The ampersand becomes a plus, lowercase becomes capitals, and the H+H logo flexes like a volume dial. Its green-yellow is now "Gello", set with Wagram serif. The new website even has pages written for AI search tools to read.

India · Identity · 1 Nov

Two Indian public bodies get new identities

Coal India unveiled a new logo on its foundation day in Kolkata, meant to show a move from coal miner to diversified energy company. Uttarakhand Police has a new flag, emblem, insignia and ceremonial uniform after about 26 years: a deep blue flag with red and gold borders, Himalayas and a river in the emblem, and a new motto, "Karm Satyam Shauryam".

Brand · 2 Nov

Apple's glowing logo comes back

Apple dropped the light-up Apple logo on its laptops in 2015. It returns on the new MacBook Pro with the PROLED display, and the launch crowd loved it, Creative Bloq says. It also helps a laptop that looks like older models stand out in public: a logo doing a product job.

UI / UX

Icons · 2 Nov

Google smooths its phone, chat and contacts icons

Google is rolling out new icons for Phone, Messages and Contacts. The shapes stay the same, but the busy overlapping lines are gone. A subtle gradient with crisp edges makes them read better on home screens and matches Google's other app icons. Nothing changes inside the apps.

Messaging · 3 Nov

WhatsApp offers your latest photo as you type

WhatsApp now suggests the newest photo or video in your gallery when you open a chat, either as a small pop-up or as a thumbnail replacing the camera icon. Tapping it opens the editor instead of sending, so mistakes don't go out by accident. It is on by default and can be switched off in settings.

iOS · 2 Nov

iPhone Messages now suggests next steps

In iOS 27.2, Messages adds small "Suggested Actions" under a message that mentions an event or a to-do, so one tap adds it to your calendar or reminders. Users can switch each of the five suggestion types on or off in Settings. It needs an Apple Intelligence iPhone.

Career · Industry · 2 Nov

Squarespace cuts staff as AI builds websites

Website builder Squarespace is laying off about 19% of its staff, around 300 people. Prompt-based "vibe-coding" site builders have shaken up the industry, and Squarespace is building its own AI platform, Forma. For students, the message is clear: typing a prompt to get a basic site is now easy, so your value is in strategy, content and taste.

Work habits · 2 Nov

Will your portfolio still open in 2046?

A Creative Bloq writer warns that digital work disappears faster than prints. Memory cards and SSDs left unpowered can start losing data within a year, and a Pew study found 38% of 2013 web pages gone a decade later. His advice: keep 3 copies on 2 kinds of storage with 1 offsite, export finals to PDF/A, SVG, PNG or TIFF, and print your best work.

Design psychology term of the day

Recognition over recall

People find it easier to recognise an option they can see than to remember it from memory. That is why menus beat typed commands for most users. WhatsApp's photo suggestion and iPhone's Suggested Actions put the next step on screen so you don't have to recall it.

Try it: find one screen in your project where users must remember something (a code, a past choice, a menu path). Show it on screen instead, and test whether people finish faster.
