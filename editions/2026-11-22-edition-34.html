Lead story · Identity · Flags · North Korea · 21 Nov

North Korea stretches its flag

The revised constitution, ratified in March and only recently published, changes the proportions of North Korea's national flag from 1:2 to 1:1.65. The government has not explained why.

What changed. The old flag used the long-standing 1:2 ratio. The new one is 1:1.65, a shape Creative Bloq says no other national flag uses. The white stripes look thicker, and the red star in its white circle now sits closer to the centre.

Why the shape matters. Most national flags use 2:3, seen in more than 80 countries. Creative Bloq reads the change as a message about power more than design. A small change in proportion can change how people read the whole symbol.

The context. The text was ratified in March, but the change only became visible once the document was published on the state website. Without an official explanation, every reading of the new shape is an informed guess.

A lesson for designers. Proportion is never neutral. The same symbol at a different ratio can feel like a different country, a different company or a different mood.

What this means for you

Check the exact ratio of any flag, emblem or official symbol before you reuse it. When you work on a client's logo, measure the proportions against the brand guide, not against memory. Any change to a symbol's shape should have a reason you can explain.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Metro Pillar Poster

Today's brief: a 2:3 pillar poster for an invented sneaker brand, CHAAL, placed on a metro station column. The Hindi line चाल बदलो sits above the English line Change your pace. It tests exact text, a fixed count of footprints, a two-colour print and a clear reading distance. No people, no real metro logos and no brands.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: design a pillar poster for your own college library. Use four footprints to show a study route. Stand 3 metres away from your screen or print, then ask a classmate to read the headline in three seconds.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 2:3 vertical transit pillar poster on a warm grey metro column. Top third: the Hindi line चाल बदलो in bold deep-red Devanagari, with the English line Change your pace below it in charcoal sans-serif. Lower half: a simple sneaker sole shape in charcoal, with four footprints in deep red stepping diagonally up the poster, one footprint per step. Brand name CHAAL in large charcoal capitals at the bottom. Flat screen print, two colours only, deep red and charcoal on warm grey. No people, no faces, no real metro logos, no QR code.

Why it works: exact text and a fixed count of footprints are easy to score. The two-colour rule shows whether the model keeps its limits.

Student Corner

Student task · 48-hour brief

Work to the same clock

Creative Minds of Tomorrow gives its teams 48 hours. Set yourself the same limit. Make one 30-second poster or reel for a fictional brand, then note what you cut to meet the deadline and why.

Design psychology term of the day

Serial position effect

People remember the first and last items in a list best, and the middle items fade. Put the message you most want kept at the start or end of a sequence.

Try it: list five features on a slide, ask a friend to recall them an hour later, then move the key feature and test again.
