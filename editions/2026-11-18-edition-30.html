Lead story · Brand proof · Redmi · Campaign India · 18 Nov

Redmi puts bad phone habits on trial

With shipments falling and prices rising, Redmi is betting that story-led creator content can make a phone's durability felt rather than claimed.

The bet. Campaign India's Inside the Ad column reads the new Redmi work as a test of proof. A durability claim on a spec sheet is easy to ignore. A creator's story shows the same claim happening to a real phone, in real use.

The open question. The column notes that the measurement question is still open. A brand can count views, but it is harder to show that a story changed what people believe about a phone's toughness. Expect this to be the debate for every campaign that relies on creators.

What this means for you

When a product claim sounds like marketing, move the proof into something the viewer can see for themselves. Before you design a campaign, write down what success looks like and how you will know. If you cannot name the test, the idea is not finished.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Setu Metro Platform Sign

Today's brief: a 16:9 platform sign for an invented Metro station, Setu Nagar (सेतु नगर), in the wayfinding style of a city transit system. Signs test what AI finds hard: exact bilingual names, arrows that point the right way, a strict line colour and consistent pictograms.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: redraw one sign from your city's transit system in Figma on a 4-column grid, then swap the line colour for an invented one. Check it at arm's length and from across a room. Which letter or arrow fails first?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 16:9 platform sign for a metro station, flat wayfinding style. Solid deep-blue band across the top with the station name in white: "सेतु नगर" in Devanagari above "Setu Nagar" in Latin letters, both sans-serif, both spelled exactly. Below, a row of three white square panels: an exit arrow pointing left, a lift pictogram, and a toilet pictogram, all drawn in the same thin-line icon style. Background: plain off-white. Only these elements. No logos, no maps, no people, no other text.

Why it works: exact names, counted arrows and one line colour make the result easy to score.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
flat wayfinding sign, metro platform, deep blue top band, white bilingual station name, three white square panels with thin-line icons: left exit arrow, lift, toilet, off-white background, flat vector, clean sans-serif --ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 100 --no people, logos, maps, extra text

V8 tip: V8 may invent extra letters. Set the names in Figma after generation and check the arrow count.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a 16:9 flat metro platform sign with a deep blue top band and three white square panels below it: exit arrow pointing left, lift icon, toilet icon. Leave the band text empty. No people, no logos.
Step 2 (same chat): Keep everything exactly the same. Write "सेतु नगर" on the top line and "Setu Nagar" below it in white sans-serif. Do not change the arrows, icons or colours.

What you are testing: whether step 2 adds bilingual text without redrawing the icons.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 16:9 metro platform sign for the invented station Setu Nagar.
Layout:
1. Top band: deep blue, full width
2. Band text: "सेतु नगर" on line 1, "Setu Nagar" on line 2, both white, sans-serif
3. Below the band: 3 white square panels in a row
4. Panel 1: exit arrow pointing left
5. Panel 2: lift icon
6. Panel 3: toilet icon
Style: flat, thin-line icons in one style, off-white background. Only these elements. No logos, maps or people.

Tip: if an arrow points the wrong way, reply "fix only the left arrow; keep everything else the same".

Design must do more than look good

Thaldev Kaim of PepsiCo argues that design goes beyond aesthetics. It shapes experiences, influences behaviour and builds cultural relevance, which is how a brand earns a lasting place in people's lives. The test for your work: does it change what someone does, not only how it looks?

AI is hitting entry-level jobs first

A Reserve Bank of Australia study finds AI has barely removed jobs overall, but job ads fell most in highly exposed fields, and young workers there are doing worse. It does not prove AI is the cause. For students, the message is clear: show judgement and craft that a tool cannot copy.

Design psychology term of the day

Zeigarnik effect

Unfinished tasks stay in memory longer than finished ones. A progress bar or a checklist keeps people going because the open loop is visible. Use it in onboarding and multi-step forms, but finish the loop quickly.

Try it: open a multi-step form you use, such as a booking or a sign-up. Mark where the progress shows, and where it does not. Which step would you redesign first?
