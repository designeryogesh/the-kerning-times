Lead story · Design tools · Canva + Figma · 4 Nov

Canva cuts deep as Figma grows 43%

On the same day, the two biggest names in everyday design software told very different stories. Canva announced the largest restructuring in its 12-year history. Figma reported revenue up 43% and said AI is now a daily habit for its biggest customers.

Canva's cuts. Creative Bloq reports that Canva will eliminate or relocate more than 1,200 jobs, almost a quarter of its global staff. The company wants to "collapse layers" and bring more teams together in person. People affected get six months' salary, 12 months of healthcare, career support, a fund for starting their own business, and they keep their MacBooks.

Why now. CEO Melanie Perkins told staff, "This has been our hardest year yet", adding that growth slowed for the first time in a decade. Creative Bloq links this to the fear that AI makes specialised software easier to replace, and notes that Adobe's share price is down about 25% in a year.

Figma's numbers. Figma's July–September revenue was $391.4 million, up 43% and above its own forecast, and it raised its full-year outlook. By 31 October, 65% of customers paying over $10,000 a year were using the Figma agent every week, and over 80% were using AI credits. Its MCP server, which links Figma files to coding tools, was the fastest-growing product, with usage up 3× in a quarter.

The bigger picture. CEO Dylan Field says "value is moving up the stack to design" as code gets cheaper. Creative Bloq, meanwhile, asks how long Canva can keep the free Affinity app free, after Remove.bg moved into Canva's paid plan.

What this means for you

Tools rise and fall faster than skills do. Don't tie your career to one app's price list: keep files portable (SVG, PDF, open formats), and invest in what moves between apps, like layout, type, systems and clear thinking. Then learn how AI agents and MCP connect design to code, because that is where Figma says its growth is coming from.

ChatGPT now makes animated GIFs

The new Images 2.6 model is rolling out to all ChatGPT and Codex users on web, desktop and mobile. You can turn a text prompt or a photo into a GIF, change its speed and edit single frames. It works because the model keeps a character consistent from frame to frame.

Spotify · AI remixing · 4 Nov

Spotify's AI remix plan should worry visual artists too

Citing the Financial Times, Creative Bloq says Spotify aims to launch AI remixing around February 2027 for $4–5 extra a month. Artists can opt in and earn royalties. The worry: if remixable work gets more reach, opting in becomes a necessity, and the same pressure could reach image platforms.

WhatsApp · Meta AI · 5 Nov

WhatsApp expands any photo to 9:16

WhatsApp beta 2.26.44.8 for Android adds an Expand option to the photo editor. Meta AI generates new content around the edges instead of stretching the image, so any photo fits a Status in seconds. Users can accept the result or try again.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Dhanteras Story Ad

Dhanteras is tomorrow, and brands will fill Instagram Stories with offers. Today's brief is a 9:16 story ad for PEETAL GHAR, an invented brass-kitchenware brand. Stories have a hard rule: the app's own buttons cover the top and bottom of the frame, so all text must sit in the middle.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: rebuild the best result in Figma at 1080 × 1920, add Instagram safe-zone guides, and replace the AI lettering with a real Devanagari typeface. Compare which version reads faster on your phone.

Version 1 · Any tool
Vertical 9:16 Instagram story ad for Dhanteras for PEETAL GHAR, an invented brass kitchenware brand. Product photo on a deep indigo backdrop. In the centre: one hand-hammered brass kalash and three copper tumblers on a low wooden chowki, one warm side light, soft reflections on the metal. Behind them, a row of small clay diyas, out of focus. Text only in the middle third: "धनतेरस" in large Devanagari, and "The Brass Edit" in small caps below. Keep the top 13% and bottom 18% of the frame plain backdrop. No coins, deities, jewellery or extra logos.

Why it works: it names the platform, gives the safe zones as percentages, counts every object and fixes where the text goes, so you can score the result line by line.

V8 tip: Devanagari is still risky in Midjourney, so --no text keeps the photo clean. Add "धनतेरस" later in Figma or Photoshop, then score test 3 on your final layout.

Graphic Design

Typeface · Swiss Post · 2 Nov

Swiss Post gets a font you shouldn't notice

Swiss Typefaces spent over three years on Swiss Post Sans. The uprights had to replace the old font without moving a single layout, so they kept its exact typographic volume, cover four national languages and use tabular figures for prices and timetables. The italics were set free: cursive a, b, e and g now act as a brand signature.

Kit design · Tottenham Hotspur · 4 Nov

An artist turns a football shirt into a story

Artist Yinka Ilori designed the club's 2026/27 away shirt: navy streaked with pink, violet, orange and electric blue, plus "THFC LDN17" for the N17 postcode. The back circles "COYS" with petals, chequered rings and lines from fan chants. Community first, football second.

Report · Koto · 3 Nov

Why every electric car brand looks the same

Studio Koto's new report says 96% of EV drivers would go electric again, but only about half stay with their brand. Electric models grew from 370 in 2020 to nearly 1,000 in 2025. In Koto's 18-point brand test, Rivian was ninth for sales but first for brand; Volkswagen was third for sales and last for brand.

UI / UX

Subscriptions · Apple · 4 Nov

Apps can now make you an offer as you cancel

Apple's Retention Messaging is live. Developers can show text, an image or a special offer inside the App Store cancellation flow, choose who sees it and how often, with up to 10 messages per app. Unlike 2024's Win-Back Offers, it reaches people before they finish cancelling. Design it as help, not a trap: one clear offer, and Cancel stays easy to find.

Android · Google Photos · 4 Nov

Google Photos may read the date on your poster

Android Authority found code in Google Photos 8.0 that lets Ask Photos pull an event's name, date, time and description from a photo of a poster and add it to your calendar. In its test, the AI assumed a US time zone the poster never mentioned and saved the event on the wrong date.

CSS · Smashing Magazine · 3 Nov

A few lines of CSS can break a whole page

Developer Shoyombo Gabriel Ayomide warns that swapping JavaScript for modern CSS, such as container queries, can shorten code but make the system harder to understand. In his example, one tidy change made a card vanish and a heading grow on tablets. New features must get along with the cascade, layout and scripts already there.

Student Corner

Career · Andstudio · 4 Nov

Start small, then go undercover

Andstudio began ten years ago in Vilnius with two designers, no clients and no plan. It now has 14 people and topped the 2025 ADC Europe ranking. For an early college-brand project, the team went undercover as students for a week. Co-founder Augustinas Paukštė says that, starting again, he would first work inside a real branding studio.

Reading pick · Creative Bloq · 4 Nov

Why you remember logo details that never existed

A University of Chicago study of the "visual Mandela effect" found people share the same false memories of logos. About 80% picked the real Apple logo, but only 20–30% got Monopoly, Volkswagen and Fruit of the Loom right, and people drew the same wrong details from memory.

Design psychology term of the day

Processing fluency

The easier something is to read or recognise, the more people tend to trust and like it, often without knowing why. Clear type, familiar layouts and steady rhythm all add fluency. Swiss Post Sans is built on this idea: a font that never makes you work.

Try it: set one paragraph from your project in a plain font and in a fancy one. Ask five people which feels more trustworthy, then ask why.
