Lead story · UI/UX · Mobile navigation · Claude · 20 Nov

Claude's phone app moves its tabs to the bottom

Anthropic has redesigned the Claude app for iPhone. Four tabs now sit at the bottom of the screen, where a thumb can reach them without a stretch.

What changed. The new bar has four tabs: Chat, Projects, Code and Artifacts. You can tap a tab or swipe along the bar to move between them. Press and hold a tab and the most recent items appear just above the bar.

Why the bar is built this way. Product designer Daniel Destefanis says the team tested the system's default tab bar against other drafts. They built a custom bar so the eye lands on the message box first, not on the navigation.

The coding flow. The home screen for coding changed too. You pick a connected device and start a new Claude Code session on it, without digging through menus.

Rollout. The design starts with iPhones on iOS 26 or later and reaches users in turn. Chats, projects and subscription plans carry over.

What this means for you

Put the action people use most within thumb reach, and let the main input stay the visual focus. Test the bar on a real phone, not only in your design file. A new navigation pattern should come with a clear way to find things that moved.

Perplexity · Monitor API · The Decoder · 22 Nov

Perplexity's watchers check the web and speak only when something changes

Developers can set a search or a page to be watched every 15 minutes to weekly. The service sends a signed webhook only when a change is relevant. Its own test found 63 fresh pages in eight hours, against 20 for Firecrawl. These are Perplexity's early internal results.

OpenAI · GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol · The Decoder · 21 Nov

Old prompts make the new models too brief

OpenAI has published copy-ready prompts for its new models. Astra writes more briefly by default, so drop 'be concise' from your brief. Ask for a final answer that stands on its own, and tell the model to act when it is already authorised.

Adobe · AI pitch · Creative Bloq · 22 Nov

Adobe says AI should support your taste, not replace it

In an interview, Adobe's Deepa Subramaniam says AI should give creatives more time for their own choices. She notes that many designers who first refused AI later adopted some of it. Ask which step of your process AI should touch, and which it should not.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Winter Reading Bookmark

Today's brief: a 1:4 bookmark for an invented poetry book, धूप की किताब (Dhoop ki Kitab). One sun-coloured tassel hangs from the top. The Hindi title sits above the English line "A book of winter light". It tests exact text, one tassel, three sun rays and a print finish. No people, no real publisher marks and no brands.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: design a bookmark for a book you love. Give it one tassel and one idea. Print it at home, then ask a classmate to name the book from two metres away.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 1:4 vertical bookmark on warm paper. A single sun-coloured tassel hangs from the top edge. Upper third: the Hindi title धूप की किताब in bold deep-orange Devanagari. Below it, the English line A book of winter light in dark brown sans-serif. Lower half: three simple sun rays in a fan shape. Flat print look, two inks only, deep orange and dark brown on warm paper. No people, no faces, no publisher logos, no QR code.

Why it works: exact Devanagari text and a fixed count of rays are easy to check by eye.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
vertical bookmark 1:4, warm paper, single sun-coloured tassel at top, Hindi title धूप की किताब in bold deep-orange Devanagari, English line A book of winter light in dark brown sans-serif, lower half three simple sun rays in a fan, flat screen print, two inks only, no people, no logos --ar 1:4 --style raw

V8 tip: Midjourney often redraws Devanagari. Set the Hindi title as live type after generation, then count the rays by eye.

Check the structure before you choose the look

Colours and type are easy to change in a template. Navigation, page layout and content order are not. Check that the template already has the sections and menus your project needs before you fall for its style.

Student Corner

Student task · Thumb reach

Map the bottom bar

Pick one app with a bottom tab bar. Sketch its tabs on a phone-sized frame. Mark where your thumb rests, then note which tabs need a stretch. Redraw the bar so the most used tab sits in the easiest spot.

Design psychology term of the day

Fitts's law

The time to reach a target depends on its distance and its size. Big buttons close to the thumb are faster to hit. Small targets far away slow people down.

Try it: time five friends as they tap a small button at the top of a screen, then a large one at the bottom. Compare the results.
