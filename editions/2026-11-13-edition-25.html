Lead story · AI · UX research · Smashing Magazine · 12 Nov

Before you add AI, ask these four questions

An AI feature can save users time on a task, then make them spend that time checking its work. UX researcher Victor Yocco's new audit helps teams decide where AI earns its place, before anyone starts designing screens.

A feature that worked, and failed. Yocco starts with a health insurer that added AI to write full decisions on benefit appeals. The technology did what it promised, yet specialists stopped using it, and those who kept using it took longer per case. Reading an AI draft carefully enough to sign it took about as long as writing one, plus hunting for mistakes they would never make.

Same tech, smaller job. Version two only pulled up the relevant policy rules and past decisions, and left the writing to the experts. The search step vanished and review time dropped. The lesson: the difference was not the model, it was which part of the task the AI took over.

The break-even line. Every interface change costs attention before it pays back: users rebuild their picture of the tool, relearn where things are and decide how far to trust the output. Yocco cites research putting working memory at about four items. Hand people an AI draft and they become checkers; for experts, that can feel like a demotion.

The four questions. One: does it cut the total effort of the task for this user? Two: does it beat how they do it today? Three: can they catch its errors? Four: after a year of use, can they still tell when it is wrong? Answer all four together while the feature is still a proposal, with at least one person who does the task for a living. A 60-minute version is included.

What this means for you

When a client asks for "an AI button", don't start with the screen. Map the task, mark where the user spends effort today, and design how they will check the AI's output: sources, highlights, an easy undo. Bring these four questions to your next brief; they turn an opinion fight into a design decision.

What an AI video clip really costs

Creative Bloq compared five AI video tools on value. Firefly Standard ($9.99 a month, 2,000 credits) covers about twenty 5-second 1080p clips, at roughly 100 credits a second, and also hosts Veo, Kling, Runway and Luma. Seedance 2.5 goes 4K, with a fast draft mode

Seedance 2.5 now renders up to 4K for 4 to 30 seconds, including Extend Video and Draft Mode. A lighter Seedance 2.5 Flash makes 480p or 720p drafts to test an idea, camera move or composition before the full render. Neither is available in the US yet.

ChatGPT ads reach 44 more markets

OpenAI named Aleph its exclusive reseller for ChatGPT Ads in 44 markets across Latin America, Europe, Central Asia, the Middle East, Africa and four Asia-Pacific countries, including Bangladesh and Sri Lanka. Brands can buy, pay and get support locally. India is not on this list.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Winter Fog Single

Fog season is here in north India. Today's brief: square cover art for "Kohra", a single by the invented indie band Neel & The Night Bus. Soft fog, a few exact lights and two scripts: AI tools often band gradients, add lamps and invent text.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: set the cover at 3000 × 3000 px in Figma or Photoshop. Then check it at 60 px, the size of a playlist thumbnail: if "KOHRA" no longer reads, make the type bigger or bolder, not the image busier.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 1:1 music single cover. A quiet Indian city road at 5 am in thick winter fog. Exactly three sodium street lamps fading into the distance, each with one soft orange halo. An empty bus stop bench on the left. Fog fades smoothly from warm grey to deep blue. Text: "KOHRA" in big thin white capitals at the top, "कोहरा" small under it, "Neel & The Night Bus" tiny at the bottom. No people, no other text.

Why it works: it names the count, the colours and every word, so each checklist point can be scored on its own.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
album cover photograph, empty Indian city road at dawn in dense winter fog, three sodium street lamps receding into the distance with soft orange halos, empty bus stop bench on the left, smooth warm grey to deep blue fog gradient, minimal, calm, large empty sky at top --ar 1:1 --style raw --s 150 --no people, faces, cars, text, letters, logos

V8 tip: keep text out and leave the top empty; add "KOHRA" and the Devanagari line yourself so the type is perfect.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Make a 1:1 photo of an empty Indian city road at dawn in thick winter fog, with exactly three sodium street lamps fading into the distance, each with one soft orange halo, and an empty bus stop bench on the left. Leave the top third as empty fog. No text, no people.
Step 2 (same chat): Keep the photo exactly the same. Add "KOHRA" in big thin white capitals in the top third, "कोहरा" small under it, and "Neel & The Night Bus" tiny at the bottom centre. Change nothing else.

What you are testing: whether step 2 keeps exactly three lamps and spells कोहरा correctly.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 1:1 single cover for "Kohra" by Neel & The Night Bus.
Layout:
1. Top third: "KOHRA" in big thin white capitals
2. Under it: "कोहरा" in small white Devanagari
3. Scene: empty Indian city road at dawn in thick fog
4. Lights: exactly 3 sodium lamps, 1 soft halo each
5. Left: one empty bus stop bench
6. Bottom centre: "Neel & The Night Bus", tiny
No people, cars, brands, logos or other text.

Tip: if the fog shows bands, reply "smooth only the fog gradient, add fine film grain, change nothing else".

Graphic Design

Rebrand · Takara Tomy · 12 Nov

A toy maker hides "play" in its logo

Tomy, maker of Pop-up Pirate and Screwball Scramble, will be called Takara Tomy everywhere, in its first rebrand in 20 years. The new symbol, in a teal-to-magenta gradient, fuses the katakana for "asobi" (play). Many people online can't decode it, and each T and A in the wordmark is drawn slightly differently. Rollout: 1 April 2027.

Rebrand · Giffgaff × Koto · 11 Nov

One letter becomes a signpost

Koto's rebrand for UK mobile network Giffgaff, which has over 4 million members, ends the final "f" in a pointed tip, a small arrow. That arrow then runs through the system, pointing to prices and next steps, even on green packing tape that reads "Your Next Move". Acid green joins the black and white.

Identity · Woven × Essential Viewing · 11 Nov

Swim, bike, run, braided into a W

To get more women into triathlon, the Ironman Group asked New Zealand studio Essential Viewing for a new community brand. Woven's symbol stacks three chevrons, one per sport, into a subtle W. The campaign line "I Am Not One Thing" skips the cheerleading tone. It has rolled out across 45 territories.

UI / UX

Figma · 3D transform · Motion · 10–12 Nov

Figma layers can now turn in 3D

A new 3D transform effect lets you rotate objects on the x, y and z axes with on-canvas handles, set perspective and depth, or ask the Figma agent to build the scene. Pair it with Figma Motion, which now has physics-based springs (mass, stiffness, damping) for iOS and Android and Lottie export previews. Hand off via CSS in Dev Mode or the Figma MCP.

Apple Music · iOS 27.3 · 11 Nov

Karaoke, now with your face

Apple demoed Selfie Sing at an Apple Music briefing in Seoul. Tap the camera on a supported song's lyrics and the front camera films you while the lyrics light up in time. You can lower the singer's vocals, trim the clip, add voice effects and share it. It was first spotted in the iOS 27.3 beta, with release due this year.

WhatsApp · Android beta 2.26.45.8 · 13 Nov

Share a place without a photo

Today, sharing a location in a WhatsApp status means picking a photo and adding a location sticker. A beta now tests a Location option right in the status composer that shows your position on a map. You can share an approximate spot or type another place. Lesson: when users fake a workflow, build it in.

Student Corner

Career · Creative Boom · 12 Nov

From bank analyst to surreal artist

Tanmay Saxena, who grew up in India's small towns, moved to London and spent a decade as a business analyst at a bank before leaving to make art. His photograph "Superhero Downtime" came from a creative block. He says ideas come from aimless walks. Lesson: a dry spell can become the work.

Design history · PRINT · 12 Nov

John Massey, Chicago modernist, dies at 95

Massey led design at Container Corporation of America and in 1964 founded its Center for Advanced Research in Design. His 1967 "Chicago Has a Great Lake" posters show what Steven Heller calls "loud without being noisy". Study his use of white space, scale and asymmetry.

Design psychology term of the day

Cognitive load

The mental effort a screen asks for. Working memory holds only about four items, so every new button, rule or AI result to verify uses up that small space. That is why an AI feature can make a task slower, not faster.

Try it: list everything a user must remember on one screen of your project. If it is more than four things, show one of them on screen instead.
