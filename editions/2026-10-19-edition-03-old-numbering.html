Lead story · App icons

Apple Music drops its red. Can an icon survive in black and white?

In the iOS 27.2 beta 4 released on 13 October, the Apple Music app icon lost its colour. It is now a black music note on a white tile, flipped to white-on-black in dark mode. Many users are not happy.

What changed. Creative Bloq describes the new icon as a flat, monochrome version of the old music note, with slightly bolder lines. The white-and-red icon it replaces had been in use since iOS 14. Before that, AppleInsider notes, the app used a multicoloured note on white from iOS 8.4 to iOS 13.

Not final yet. This is a beta, so it is not confirmed that the black-and-white icon will reach everyone when iOS 27.2 ships to the public. Apple has not explained the change. The same beta also quietly pulled the redesigned Health app that earlier test builds had shown.

Mixed signals. Creative Bloq points out that Apple has lately been more playful, with brighter, youth-focused branding, and a leaked new Apple Music wordmark. A colourless icon sits oddly next to that. One user on X called the new look "miserable". Another guessed that a plain mark lets artists bring their own colour to billboard ads.

Why designers should care. Colour does a lot of work on a phone's home screen. Many people find apps by colour before they read a single label. Remove the red, and the shape alone has to do the job of being recognised among dozens of other icons.

What this means for you

Every logo and app icon you design should pass a one-colour test. Fill your mark in pure black on white, shrink it to the size of a fingernail and put it next to ten other icons. If people can still pick it out, the shape is strong. If they can't, the colour was hiding a weak idea. Students: do this with your portfolio logo today, then try a white-on-black version for dark mode.

AI Desk

India · 16 Oct

ChatGPT now speaks Hindi in a festive campaign

OpenAI's new Indian film series follows the Kumar family, whose ancestral land is claimed by a developer, as they use ChatGPT through the festive season. It shows off two Hindi voices, Neer and Leher, added with GPT Live 1.1. A useful reference if you write scripts for regional AI ads.

Vision · 16 Oct

Glimpse: an AI that zooms into images while it thinks

Palo Alto startup Elorian released Glimpse as a research preview. Instead of describing a picture once, it keeps going back to it: cropping regions, pulling video frames or running code to measure things. API price: US$0.60 per million input tokens and US$3 per million output. Benchmarks are the company's own.

Video · 15 Oct

A sharper FLUX 3 video upscaler on Higgsfield

A new build of Black Forest Labs' FLUX 3 Video Upscaler replaced the old one automatically. Higgsfield says it gives sharper detail, keeps motion and audio, runs faster and uses fewer credits per upscale. For now it is only on Higgsfield.

API · 13 Oct

Kling models arrive on the Runway API

Developers can now call Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro, Kling 3.0 and Kling O3 through Runway's API. Kling 3.0 makes 3 to 15-second clips with up to 6 shots per prompt, and O3 takes up to 7 reference images. Prices start at 9 credits per second.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Kandil Workshop Poster

Diwali is on 8 November, and housing societies across Maharashtra will soon host akash kandil (paper sky lantern) workshops. Yesterday we made a 4:5 sale post; today it is a printed A3 event poster. The hard parts for AI: a symmetric star lantern, believable paper folds, glowing light and one word in Devanagari.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: keep only the AI lantern image. Rebuild the poster in Illustrator or Figma at A3 with 3 mm bleed: set "आकाशकंदील" in a real Devanagari typeface, make the date the second loudest thing, and print one copy in college. Pin the AI version and yours side by side and ask five people which one makes them want to attend.

Version 1 · Any tool
Portrait A3 event poster for "Kandil Katta", an invented community art club in Thane, India, announcing a Diwali sky-lantern workshop. Hero: one glowing eight-pointed star paper lantern (akash kandil) in saffron and magenta paper with tissue tails, hanging from a thread at the top centre, lit from inside. Background: a deep indigo evening wall with the lantern's warm glow and soft star-shaped shadows. Bold condensed headline at the top: "KANDIL KATTA". Under the lantern, in Devanagari: "आकाशकंदील". Bottom: "1–2 NOV · 4–7 PM". Keep a wide margin. Paper-craft photography with flat graphic type. No other text, no logo, no watermark.

Why it works: it names the exact lantern shape, the light source and every word allowed. Symmetry, glow and non-Latin script are three classic AI weak spots, so each one is easy to score.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
Diwali workshop event poster, one glowing eight-pointed star paper sky lantern in saffron and magenta paper with tissue tails hanging from a thread, lit from within, deep indigo evening wall with warm glow and star-shaped shadows, bold condensed headline "KANDIL KATTA", Devanagari text "आकाशकंदील", small text "1-2 NOV 4-7 PM", wide margins, paper craft photography with flat graphic typography --ar 5:7 --raw --stylize 100

V8 tip: --ar 5:7 is close to A-series paper. If the Devanagari comes out as squiggles, that is a fail to note, not a reason to reroll 20 times: set that word yourself in Illustrator.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a portrait 5:7 photo of one glowing eight-pointed star paper lantern (akash kandil) in saffron and magenta paper with tissue tails, hanging from a thread at the top centre, lit from inside, against a deep indigo wall with warm glow and star-shaped shadows. No text. Thinking level: high.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the lantern, light and shadows exactly the same. Add a bold condensed headline "KANDIL KATTA" at the top, the Devanagari word "आकाशकंदील" under the lantern, and "1–2 NOV · 4–7 PM" small at the bottom. Keep a wide margin on all sides.

What you are testing: whether adding type makes the model redraw the lantern. Compare step 1 and step 2: if the number of points or the shadow shape changes, it fails test 4 or 5.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a portrait A3 event poster for "Kandil Katta", an invented art club in Thane, India, for a Diwali sky-lantern workshop.

Layout, top to bottom:
1. Headline, bold condensed caps: KANDIL KATTA
2. Centre: one glowing eight-pointed star paper lantern, saffron and magenta, hanging from a thread, lit from inside
3. Under the lantern, in Devanagari: आकाशकंदील
4. Bottom, small: 1–2 NOV · 4–7 PM

Background: deep indigo wall with warm glow and star-shaped shadows. Wide margin. No other text.

Tip: a numbered layout keeps GPT Image's text in order. If line 3 is wrong, reply "fix only line 3, keep everything else" and check the matras again.

Graphic Design

Identity · 14 Oct

Two dots give a festival a face

Vilnius agency andstudio rebranded Berlin's Pictoplasma character design festival with one idea: two dots used as eyes, so the lettering becomes a face. The dots are a typed glyph in a custom version of Lift Type's Melun, so anyone can set the logo. Colours change each edition. Tickets sold out six weeks early.

India · Rebrand · 16 Oct

ICICI Prudential Life becomes ICICI Life

One of India's big insurers has a shorter name and a new Hindi tagline, "Har Kal Aasaan". The company says buying life insurance has felt complicated, and the new brand is about making planning for tomorrow simpler. A good case study in how much a name and a tagline can carry.

Social ad · 17 Oct

IKEA answers a flying car with a towel

After Tesla's livestreamed flying-car reveal, IKEA Taiwan posted its own version on Instagram: a set of Lillabo toy cars riding a Brokglim bath towel held up like a track, captioned "Safe, affordable." Fast, cheap and on brand. Reactive posts work when the joke is simple.

UI / UX

Tools · 14 Oct

Figma puts real code on the canvas

Code layers, now in open beta, let you place working, code-backed prototypes inside a Figma file and turn them back into editable frames. They use AI credits, but creating and editing them is free on paid plans until 4 November. Figma also opened Specs, a shared place for product context that coding agents can read.

Research · 16 Oct

29 UX maps, finally untangled

Nielsen Norman Group's new glossary explains 29 maps, from journey maps and service blueprints to wireflows and heatmaps. Its key tip: an experience map shows general human behaviour, a journey map is about your product. Choose the map by your goal, not by habit.

Product · 13 Oct

Apple holds back its AI Health redesign

The redesigned Health app, with AI summaries and suggestions, has vanished from iOS 27.2 beta 4, says AppleInsider. A message now says it is coming later this year. A reminder for product teams: AI features in sensitive areas like health need more testing, and a delay beats a bad launch.

Baltimore designer Sewell Killingstad told It's Nice That that every dream brief came from personal work posted online. Design psychology term of the day

Pareidolia

Our brain's habit of seeing faces where there are none: two dots and a line become eyes and a mouth. Pictoplasma's rebrand uses exactly this, which is why two dots feel alive. It is also why a plug socket can look surprised.

Try it: add two simple dots to a word or object in your next poster or mascot sketch. If people smile, pareidolia is working for you.
