Lead story · Title design · Film · 7 Nov

The film title card is back as a design moment

A title card used to be a few seconds of white type on black. In 2026, filmmakers are treating it like key art: hand-made, stitched, glitched and woven into the scene. Creative Bloq calls this the year of the movie title card.

Made by hand. Creative Bloq's Natalie Fear picks six openers from this year. "28 Years Later: The Bone Temple" uses a loose, hand-drawn scrawl in blood red, a quiet beat after a violent opening that feels nothing like the film's clean sans-serif poster. Laika's "Wildwood" builds each letter from real wood textures, crawling ivy and pigment, which she reads as a rejection of AI slop.

Made from strange materials. "Wuthering Heights" made its stop-motion title from hair, including some of the lead actors' own. Director Emerald Fennell says, "We wanted it to feel completely human."

Type that sets the mood. "The Drama" flashes a jumbled mix of scripts, serifs and bold sans so fast it looks like a glitch, planting anxiety in a calm dance scene. "Obsession" hides its title in the pattern of a duvet, so the horror feels close to home.

Sound matters too. "Resident Evil" dropped the game's famous voice-over and let a thumping score and fast-warping bold type do the work. Fans argued, but the article says the choice made the film feel raw rather than campy.

What this means for you

If you design title treatments for Indian films, OTT shows or YouTube series, treat the title card as storytelling, not decoration. Ask what material the story is made of (rangoli powder, chalk, embroidery, neon) and build the letters from it. Then test it in motion and with sound, because the title card lives on screen, not on a poster.

A video model rebuilt as a fast image maker

Reshoot a finished clip without reshooting it

Viggle Remake re-renders existing video: swap the people for other characters, or change objects, weather and light, while the motion, camera and sound stay as they were. Use it only on footage you own or have permission to change.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Diwali Night Title Card

Today's lead is about film title cards, and it is Diwali night. So today's brief is a title treatment: the opening title of an invented short film, made from rangoli powder on a courtyard floor. AI tools love clean fonts; can they make lettering that looks hand-poured?

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: recreate the title for real with salt, rice or rangoli colours on black chart paper, photograph it from above, then animate a 5-second reveal in After Effects. Compare your hand-made version with the AI one.

Version 1 · Any tool
A 16:9 film title card for an invented short film, shot straight down from above. A dark stone courtyard floor at night. In the centre, the title "EK RAAT" is formed by hand from loose marigold-orange and white rangoli powder, with uneven, slightly scattered edges. Below it, smaller, "एक रात" in white powder. Three small clay diyas sit in a gentle curve under the text, each with one flame, giving warm light. Bottom right, tiny and neat: "a Ghar Ghar Pictures film". Moody, cinematic, lots of dark empty space, no people.

Why it works: it names the material, the camera angle and the exact words, so you can see whether the tool builds letters from powder or just pastes a font on top.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
top-down cinematic film title card, dark stone courtyard at night, the words "EK RAAT" hand-poured in marigold orange and white rangoli powder, uneven powdery edges, three clay diyas with single flames in a soft curve below, warm low light, deep shadows, wide empty space --ar 16:9 --style raw --no people, hands, fireworks

V8 tip: V8 can render short English words, but check every letter. Add the Devanagari line and the credit yourself in After Effects; never trust a tool with credits.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Create a 16:9 top-down photo of a dark stone courtyard floor at night with three clay diyas in a soft curve near the bottom, one flame each. Leave the centre empty.

Step 2 (same chat): Keep the floor, diyas and light exactly the same. In the centre, pour the title "EK RAAT" in loose marigold-orange and white rangoli powder, and below it "एक रात" in white powder. Add "a Ghar Ghar Pictures film" small at the bottom right.

What you are testing: whether step 2 keeps the powder texture real and the diya count at three, and whether the Hindi line stays correct next to English.

Version 4 · ChatGPT
Create a 16:9 film title card, camera looking straight down.

Layout:
1. Background: dark stone courtyard, night
2. Centre: "EK RAAT" in hand-poured rangoli powder
3. Colours: marigold orange + white only
4. Below: "एक रात" in white powder
5. Under that: 3 clay diyas, 1 flame each
6. Bottom right, small: "a Ghar Ghar Pictures film"
7. Warm light from the diyas only

No people, hands, fireworks or other text.

Tip: if the letters look like a clean font, reply "make the edges powdery and uneven, like real rangoli, change nothing else".

Design psychology term of the day

Law of Prägnanz

People read shapes in the simplest way they can. When a mark is complex, the eye tries to reduce it to one clear form; if it cannot, the mark feels confusing. That is today's volleyball-logo problem: five ideas, no single simple shape.

Try it: sketch a logo idea, then show it to three friends for two seconds. Ask what they saw. If they name different things, simplify.
