Lead story · Brand identity · Sutherland Shire Council · Creative Bloq · 14 Nov

A logo is a tool, not a monument

A Sydney council began using a simple wave logo for everyday jobs like email signatures and kept its old crest for ceremonies. The switch still became a national political fight, and that should worry every designer who plans a refresh.

A small change, a big fight. Sutherland Shire Council, in Sydney's south, serves about 240,000 people. Its crest carries an engraved portrait of Captain James Cook, who landed at Kurnell, inside the shire, in 1770. Since July, staff have preferred a newer light-blue wave mark for day-to-day communication.

Nothing was removed. The council moved the crest to civic, ceremonial and high-value uses, and used the wave where a simple mark works better, such as email signatures. A councillor who was not consulted tabled a motion to review the policy and took the issue to national breakfast TV. Others piled in, and a logo became part of Australia's culture wars.

The history nobody noticed. The identity had changed quietly before: in 2008 the council shrank the portrait in favour of its name and new colours, in 2017 it introduced the wave, and ahead of the landing's 250th anniversary it restyled the portrait for screens. None of it made national news.

Why it matters. Creative Bloq's Tom May argues that logos need maintenance when they look tired or fail on small screens, and points to quiet updates by Mastercard, Volkswagen, Burger King and Pepsi. His worry: after rows like this one, many clients may decide it is safer never to change anything, and get stuck with identities that no longer work.

What this means for you

Before you present a refresh, map where each mark will live (ceremony, signage, app icon, email) and show that old and new can work side by side. Add a one-page "what is not changing" note for stakeholders. People react to a feeling of loss before they look at the design, so name what stays first.

AI Desk

Dior · AI row update · 12 Nov

Dior's director: interpolation, not generative AI

Sylvain Chomet, who directed Dior's holiday film, denied using generative AI after viewers spotted six-fingered hands and morphing details. His producer said 20 artists worked for seven months, and that software drawing in-between frames was only a small part. The anger was mostly about the "drawn by hand" claim. Lesson: say clearly how you made the work.

Anthropic · Prompting · 14 Nov

Stop telling AI to "think hard"

Anthropic says old prompt habits slow down Claude Opus 5.5. Its tips: drop "think hard", start at Medium effort, describe the finished result in one message, attach diagrams as images, name the design patterns you don't want and share your own house style. In its tests, replies started faster with no measured quality drop.

Higgsfield · GPU rental · 13 Nov

Rent a GPU by chatting with an agent

Higgsfield now lets you rent a cloud GPU from a chat in Claude, ChatGPT or Supercomputer. The agent installs the software and runs jobs such as rendering a Blender animation or using a LoRA you paste from Hugging Face or Civitai. You pay by time, it auto-pauses after 10 idle minutes, and you must export results before stopping.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Thekua Recipe Card

Happy Chhath. Today's brief: an illustrated 4:5 recipe card for thekua, the crisp jaggery-wheat cookie made for Chhath, for the invented brand Ghat Ghar Kitchen. Step-by-step cards test what AI finds hard: numbered order, counts, repeated patterns and Devanagari.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: rebuild the card in Figma on a 1080 × 1350 frame with an 8-column grid. Set ठेकुआ in Tiro Devanagari Hindi or Mukta and keep every step under 12 words. Then hand it to a friend: can they follow it without asking you a single question?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 4:5 recipe card for Ghat Ghar Kitchen, flat gouache on cream paper in terracotta, jaggery brown and leaf green. Top: "ठेकुआ" hand-lettered, "Thekua · Festive Wheat Cookies" small below. A row of 5 ingredient icons: wheat flour, jaggery, ghee, coconut, fennel. Then 4 numbered panels (2 × 2): jaggery melting in a brass pan, dough in a steel bowl, a wooden mould pressing a pattern, cookies frying in a kadhai. Bottom: banana leaf with 6 cookies, same carved pattern on each. Tiny "GHAT GHAR KITCHEN" bottom right. No people, hands, deities or other text.

Why it works: every count and position is written down, so you can score panel by panel.

Version 2 · Midjourney V8
flat gouache recipe card, cream paper, terracotta, jaggery brown and leaf green, 2 by 2 grid of cooking panels, brass pan of melting jaggery, steel bowl of dough, carved wooden cookie mould, cookies frying in a kadhai, five ingredient icons on top, banana leaf with six identical patterned cookies --ar 4:5 --style raw --stylize 150 --no text, letters, people, hands, logos

V8 tip: make the art without text, then add ठेकुआ and the step numbers yourself; check that V8 keeps exactly four panels.

Version 3 · Nano Banana
Step 1: Make the card from Version 1 with no text at all, leaving space at the top for a title. No people, no hands.
Step 2 (same chat): Keep everything exactly the same. Add "ठेकुआ" at the top, "Thekua · Festive Wheat Cookies" below, numbers 1–4 in the panel corners, labels Atta, Gur, Ghee, Coconut, Saunf under the icons and tiny "GHAT GHAR KITCHEN" bottom right.

What you are testing: whether step 2 adds text without redrawing the panels or changing the cookie pattern.

Identity · Colorplan × Bibliothèque · 9 Nov

A paper brand draws on its machines

GF Smith's Colorplan paper range has a new global identity by London studio Bibliothèque. The team looked back to Plan 8, the 1960s range that came before Colorplan, and drew a new wordmark and symbol from the way paper moves through production machinery. The tagline: "Create What's Next".

Mascot · Plusnet · 10 Nov

A fluffy pink plus sign sells value

UK broadband brand Plusnet rebuilt its whole identity with in-house agency The Exchange and a Publicis Groupe team: colour, type, logo, icons, motion, tone of voice and sound. The star is Plusso, a fluffy pink plus-sign mascot who shares deals, introduced through social-first "audition tape" videos under "Massive Value. Massive Plus".

Animation · Pixar's Gatto · 14 Nov

Pixar tries a painted look

The full trailer for Pixar's Gatto (in cinemas 5 March 2027) shows its hybrid style: sets and characters are built and animated in 3D for depth, light and camera moves, then given hand-painted 2D textures and a flatter perspective. Many viewers like the storybook feel; others say Pixar didn't go far enough.

Text stickers you can actually find

Pixel · Android 17 bug · 13 Nov

Dark mode reveals a hidden space

A bug first seen in September survived Google's November Pixel update: switching between light and dark mode makes a hidden Private Space reappear in the app drawer. Scheduled theme changes can trigger it without the owner noticing. Test every state change, not just every screen.

Journey mapping, from the basics

NN/g's updated Journey Mapping 101 lists five parts of a map: one actor, a scenario with expectations, journey phases, the actor's actions, thoughts and emotions, and opportunities at the end. Careers · Creative Boom · 10 Nov

Illustration has no finish line

Dutch illustrator Hanneke Rozemuller, an HKU graduate who now draws for Wired, The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal, says she once expected illustration to feel like arriving somewhere. Now she sees "a practice without a finish line", and is proudest of the work where her own strangeness shows most.

Design psychology term of the day

Status quo bias

Our habit of preferring things as they are, and of feeling any change as a loss, even when nothing is taken away. It is why a council's new email logo can spark a row while the old crest is still in use.

Try it: presenting a rebrand? Show old and new side by side in real places, and list what stays the same before you show what changes.
