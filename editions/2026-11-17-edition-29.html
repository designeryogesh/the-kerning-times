Lead story · Brand character · Reset · Creative Boom · 16 Nov

A weight-loss app finds its voice in a block character

Reset wants to feel less like a nagging aunt counting calories. Studio Kiln built its new identity around Ester, a faceless character of soft 3D blocks that speaks through movement rather than expressions.

The brief. Reset, first launched in 2019, is a weight-loss app for people who would rather not count every calorie. It starts by working out your metabolic type and then adapts its guidance. The identity had to show that calm, personal approach without a single preachy line of copy.

A character with no face. Studio Kiln, based in Cornwall and London, made Ester from soft, rounded blocks: a head-like shape with one circular eye, a small floating dot and two chunky legs. Put together, the blocks form the letter R for Reset.

Movement does the talking. Ester rarely stays still. As the user moves through the app, the blocks revolve, tumble, shift and rearrange, falling apart and coming back together. The character reacts to the user's progress instead of smiling or frowning at them.

Why it matters. Most health apps rely on guilt, streaks and red numbers. A friendly, abstract guide lets the brand sound supportive without sounding like a judge. The press notes describe Ester as using "they" pronouns, a small choice that adds to the sense of a guide, not a coach.

What this means for you

A character does not need a face to carry emotion. Try building a mascot out of one shape and let its motion, timing and rhythm show mood. Then test it at small sizes, because the motion is the first thing a phone screen will lose.

Prompt of the day · Test it, score it, then redesign it

The Auto Back-Panel

Today's brief: a 2:1 back-panel advertisement for an invented cab service, NUKKAD RIDES, on a three-wheeler in winter fog. The line is "Ghar tak, bina wait ke" (door to door, no waiting). It tests what AI finds hard: exact Devanagari, one clear scene and a readable message at a distance.

Score: 0 / 5

Student task: put your best result on a 2:1 mock vehicle in Figma. Then shrink it to a 1-inch width and check: can you still read the Hindi line from the road, and do the lamps still look like three separate lights?

Version 1 · Any tool
A 2:1 back-panel advertisement on a Mumbai-style three-wheeler, winter fog in the street behind it. Three street lamps in the fog, each with one soft halo, the only light sources in the scene. On the panel, large Devanagari text reads "घर तक, बिना वेटिंग के" in black on taxi yellow. Below it, small black text reads "NUKKAD RIDES". Colours: taxi yellow, ink black and one white. No people, no faces, no phone numbers, no real brand logos.

Why it works: one scene, counted lamps and exact text make the result easy to score.

No new student story was verified today. Use the time for your portfolio. Open it on a phone, look at the first and last page, and ask whether a stranger knows your strongest work within ten seconds.

Design psychology term of the day

Serial position effect

People remember the first and last items in a list best, and forget the middle. It is why a menu, a navigation bar or a portfolio should put its strongest item at the start or the end.

Try it: list your five main navigation items. Move your best one to the first or last spot, then ask a friend to recall the list after ten seconds.
